# Summary

Do the saved models improve follow-up infarct prediction, and what explains their gains? In 99 development patients, fusion improved placement at fixed predicted volume: Dice rose from 0.2063 to 0.2467, a gain of 0.0404 (95% interval 0.0280–0.0542). Smoothing greatly reduced fragmentation, but fusion's advantage over equally processed nnU-Net remains uncertain. Small infarcts remain poorly predicted; regional evidence is missing. Three hardware benchmarks measured first epochs of 278, 228 and 166 seconds; neither five-epoch smoke established steady-state speed. These results support retaining promising models and separating ranking, volume and topology, rather than choosing by Dice alone. Reused development outcomes, incomplete admission-input provenance and unmatched timing boundaries limit interpretation. Item6's prior approval and the smoke review's REVISE remain distinct. Stop this synthesis here; the candidate tests and private case-selection plan remain proposals.

**What the comparisons actually isolate.** For prediction P containing k voxels and truth G, Dice = 2|P∩G|/(k+|G|). Within a patient, fixing k and G makes a Dice increase an overlap increase. Item6's 594 equal-count checks and zero infeasible common-domain cases (V013) support this reading. They cannot identify a better amount estimator. Original self-volume fusion, union, thresholding and the fixed-amount smoothed rule are different interventions.

Item6 uses tissue support union nnU-Net probability >0.0001 for its primary; Sprint14's primary uses tissue support. Sprint14's wider-domain sensitivity is unsmoothed, so comparing it with item6's smoothed primary would conflate domain and smoothing. The common-support result weakens a domain-expansion-only explanation. Sprint14's claim that domain “does not matter” is stronger than its limited sensitivity warrants (V003 “Differences” 1; V029; V037).

Both use 2,000 patient bootstrap draws with seed0, but patient ordering and undefined-value handling differ. Colab samples all patients then uses nanmean; item6 filters undefined endpoints first. Thus the common-support F1 interval's rounded Colab lower bound +0.000 is not a replicated positive result: item6 includes a negative lower bound. V003 also distinguishes native threshold-mask agreement 1.0 from Colab's after-resampling comparison 0.9187. Those test different operations. Calibration volume errors use within-domain truth in item6 versus whole-grid truth in Colab, and means versus medians; they are not interchangeable endpoints.

**Ranked candidate ideas, with tests that could disprove them.** These are future candidates; completed item6 diagnostics are prior legwork, not proposed reruns.

1. **Inspect the topology tradeoff using the fixed visual sample below.** Computer-science mechanism: Gaussian smoothing suppresses local isolated peaks before top-k selection, potentially exchanging scattered voxels for coherent overlap.
