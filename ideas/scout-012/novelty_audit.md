# Novelty audit — scouting cycle 012

## C1 — The race signal in chest CT: measure the bone density everyone names and nobody measured (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Gichoya et al., “AI recognition of patient race in medical imaging: a modelling study,” *Lancet Digital Health* 2022, DOI `10.1016/S2589-7500(22)00063-2`: established high race-prediction performance in NLST chest CT and tested frequency, anatomy, image quality, BMI, disease, and radiographic bright-pixel/bone suppression, but did not quantify CT vertebral attenuation. (ii) Beheshtian et al., “Ability of artificial intelligence to identify self-reported race in chest X-ray using pixel intensity counts,” *Journal of Digital Imaging* 2023, PMCID `PMC10400898`: tested intensity-count and acquisition-variable explanations for race prediction on chest radiographs, including a bone-range intensity bin, but not calibrated trabecular CT HU or a model intervention. (iii) Yi et al., “Using generative AI to investigate medical imagery models and datasets,” *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association* 2024, PMCID `PMC10993140` (preprint arXiv `2306.00985`): used generated counterfactual chest radiographs and expert review to propose visually interpretable race-associated features, including skeletal conspicuity, but did not measure vertebral attenuation on CT or test whether a race classifier uses it.
2. **Delta.** This candidate quantitatively measures vertebral trabecular HU in the same CT modality and cohort in which race prediction was demonstrated, then tests model use with trabecular-only substitution rather than inferring mechanism from filtering, saliency, or generated-image appearance.
3. **Why not done — BLIND_SPOT.** Race-decoding work has been framed as broad shortcut/confound elimination, while opportunistic-osteoporosis work treats vertebral HU as a clinical endpoint; the disciplinary boundary left the calibrated CT measurement unconnected to the race classifier.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE.** Searches spanning the anchor paper, subsequent race-interpretability studies, counterfactual imaging, pixel-intensity studies, and CT-BMD literature found close neighbors but no quantitative vertebral-HU analysis or intervention on a chest-CT race classifier.

## C2 — The dilated esophagus inside the fibrosis score (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Richardson et al., “Esophageal dilatation and interstitial lung disease in systemic sclerosis: A cross-sectional study,” *Seminars in Arthritis and Rheumatism* 2016, DOI `10.1016/j.semarthrit.2016.02.004`: showed widest esophageal diameter on HRCT was associated with fibrosis/ILD severity and pulmonary function in systemic sclerosis. (ii) Winstone et al., “Oesophageal diameter is associated with severity but not progression of systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease,” *Respirology* 2018, PMID `29641847`: related esophageal diameter and hiatal hernia to baseline SSc-ILD severity but not independently to progression. (iii) de Figueiredo et al., “Esophageal Dilatation as a Predictor of Systemic Sclerosis in Patients with Interstitial Lung Disease,” *Turkish Thoracic Journal* 2021, PMCID `PMC8975317`: evaluated esophageal diameter as a diagnostic discriminator for systemic sclerosis among ILD patients. A model-side neighbor, Goetze et al., “Prediction Degeneracy in Medical Vision-Language Models,” ICCV VLM3D Workshop 2025, examined CT-CLIP prediction degeneracy, but not esophageal reliance of its fibrosis head.
2. **Delta.** Prior studies establish that esophageal dilation covaries with SSc-ILD; this candidate instead asks whether a released CT vision-language model's fibrosis score causally uses the esophageal air column through a sham-controlled within-volume edit.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** The released CT-CLIP/CT-RATE model and its reproducible 18-head checkpoint (Hamamci et al., arXiv `2403.17834`) newly provide a named fibrosis score that can be queried before and after an annotation-free organ-specific edit.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE.** Multi-source searches found extensive esophagus–ILD association literature and general CT-CLIP robustness work, but no study testing a fibrosis model's reliance on esophageal dilation or any analogous esophagus-specific intervention.

## C3 — Merlin's COPD call may come from the lungs it wasn't asked to look at (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Blankemeier et al., “Merlin: A Computed Tomography Vision-Language Foundation Model and Dataset,” *Nature* 2026, DOI `10.1038/s41586-026-10181-8` / arXiv `2406.06512`: released an abdominal-CT foundation model with phenotype classification and chronic-disease prediction, but did not identify which anatomy supports respiratory outputs. (ii) Gryak et al., “Detecting Underdiagnosed Medical Conditions with Deep Learning-Based Opportunistic CT Imaging,” arXiv `2409.11686`: used abdominal CT for opportunistic detection of underdiagnosed conditions, establishing the general cross-organ opportunity but not lung-base emphysema use by Merlin. (iii) Schroeder et al., “Relationships between airflow obstruction and quantitative CT measurements of emphysema, air trapping, and airways in subjects with and without chronic obstructive pulmonary disease,” *AJR* 2013, PMID `23971478`: established whole-lung `%LAA-950` and airway measures as correlates of spirometric obstruction, but did not study partial lung-base coverage or a foundation model.
2. **Delta.** This candidate varies only naturally plausible superior coverage of the same abdominal CT and attributes Merlin's paired COPD-score change to the `%LAA-950` content removed, rather than merely predicting COPD from a full chest scan or an abdominal scan.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** Merlin's newly released phenotype head, weights, and variable-coverage abdominal-CT pipeline make it possible to interrogate a specific respiratory output of an abdominal foundation model; before Merlin there was no public target model with this combination.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH.** Searches found Merlin, quantitative emphysema/COPD studies, and opportunistic abdominal-CT prediction, but no lung-base field-of-view ablation of Merlin or another abdominal foundation model. The verdict is limited because “lung bases on abdominal CT” terminology is inconsistent and recent Merlin follow-up work is still sparse.

## C4 — The non-gated chest CT contains an ECG: heart rate written in motion banding (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Kalisz et al., “Artifacts at Cardiac CT: Physics and Solutions,” *RadioGraphics* 2016, DOI `10.1148/rg.2016160079`: related cardiac motion artifacts to heart rate, temporal resolution, reconstruction, and ECG editing, treating artifacts as image degradation. (ii) Kachelrieß et al., “Artifacts in Cardiac Computed Tomographic Images,” *Journal of the American College of Radiology* 2009, PMCID `PMC6360940`: described cardiac banding/step artifacts and their dependence on nonperiodic heart motion and gating/reconstruction. (iii) Leschka et al., “Influence of heart rate and phase of the cardiac cycle on the occurrence of motion artifact in dual-source CT angiography of the coronary arteries,” *Journal of Cardiovascular Computed Tomography* 2012, exact title/venue/year: experimentally linked heart rate and cardiac phase to coronary motion artifact. These works concern gated cardiac CT image quality, not heart-rate recovery from routine nongated chest CT or model reliance on the artifact.
2. **Delta.** This candidate treats the spatial periodicity of nongated helical cardiac-border artifact as a quantitative, metadata-inverted heart-rate measurement and asks whether CT-CLIP cardiac scores use it; the neighbors only characterize or suppress artifact severity.
3. **Why not done — BLIND_SPOT.** Cardiac-CT physics treats banding as a nuisance to minimize, whereas medical-AI interpretation treats anatomy as signal; neither field is incentivized to turn a nuisance artifact into a physiological sensor and then probe a model with it.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH.** Searches across cardiac CT artifact physics, heart-rate effects, motion correction, physiological-signal extraction, and CT-CLIP found no recovery of heart rate from nongated helical banding and no model-use test. Confidence remains limited because the proposed beat-frequency inversion itself has not been validated and terminology overlaps with unrelated MRI “banding.”

## C5 — The prognosis model as a manometer: midline shift is pressure the skull wrote down (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Pease et al., “Outcome Prediction in Patients with Severe Traumatic Brain Injury Using Deep Learning from Head CT Scans,” *Radiology* 2022, DOI `10.1148/radiol.212181`: built the exact CT-plus-clinical six-month outcome model this candidate would decode, but did not quantitatively decompose its image signal into midline shift and lesion burden. (ii) Jacobs et al., “Computed Tomography and Outcome in Moderate and Severe Traumatic Brain Injury: Hematoma Volume and Midline Shift Revisited,” *Journal of Neurotrauma* 2011, DOI `10.1089/neu.2010.1558`: jointly analyzed lesion volume and midline shift as outcome predictors. (iii) “Quantification of midline shift as a predictor of poor outcome following head injury,” *Surgical Neurology* 1991, PII `0090-3019(91)90069-L` (exact title/venue/year): specifically reported that shift out of proportion to intracranial hemorrhage predicted poor outcome. A measurement neighbor, Jain et al., “Automatic Quantification of Computed Tomography Features in Acute Traumatic Brain Injury,” *Journal of Neurotrauma* 2019, PMCID `PMC6551991`, automated lesion volume, cistern volume, and midline shift.
2. **Delta.** The biological/prognostic construct is not new; the candidate's narrower delta is to test whether Pease's end-to-end image model internally uses the already-known shift-versus-lesion-volume relationship rather than merely showing that the relationship predicts outcome.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** The Pease end-to-end prognostic model supplies the object to decode, while validated automated TBI CT quantification now supplies lesion, cistern, and shift measurements at scale; model access remains the unresolved capability gate.
4. **Verdict — INCREMENTAL.** The proposed X and even “shift out of proportion to hemorrhage” are established prior prognostic work; decoding the Pease model is a legitimate but incremental model-interpretation delta. If model scores/weights are unavailable, recommend kill rather than substituting a newly trained model.
