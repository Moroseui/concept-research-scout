# Debate transcript

## Round 1 — CRITIC

**Position:** The proposed partial regression and paired-kernel analysis cannot support the deliverable sentence that Sybil is using coronary artery calcium; they identify correlation with CAC and generic reconstruction-sensitive image signal, respectively.

**Argument:** CAC is a marker of a diffuse age/smoking/vascular phenotype that also includes aortic calcium, vertebral degeneration, airway disease, cardiac morphology, body-habitus-dependent noise, and acquisition differences. Conditioning on pack-years, LAA-950, and BV5 only removes the measured components of three selected covariates; it does not turn the remaining AI-CAC coefficient into evidence that Sybil reads coronary lesions. The paired-kernel arm is no stronger: changing kernel jointly changes calcium blooming, emphysema thresholds, edges, and texture throughout the volume, so correlated changes in AI-CAC and Sybil score demonstrate reconstruction sensitivity, not CAC-specific use. This is fatal under the charter because the candidate asserts rung 3 from associative evidence. A targeted within-scan intervention on AI-CAC lesion masks could preserve the core question, but only if realistic inpainting holds all non-CAC content fixed and is compared with size- and attenuation-matched edits in noncalcified coronary regions and noncoronary high-density lesions; simple deletion would repeat idea 006's out-of-distribution failure. Moving that experiment to an ordinary NLST subset would test a general Sybil mechanism, not the original claim about the published future-cancer-site-nodule-excluded residual, so that amendment would fail the identity check unless the title, question, and claim are explicitly narrowed or the replacement becomes a separate candidate.

**What would change my mind:** Show a preregistered, fidelity-validated within-scan CAC inpainting experiment on a reproducibly obtainable version of the stated future-cancer-site-nodule-excluded cohort, with the two matched control edits above and a positive-control model demonstrably sensitive to CAC; the Sybil score change must be specifically larger for coronary-calcium removal than for both controls.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 1 — PROPOSER

The only repair that preserves the question is a within-scan, fidelity-validated CAC inpainting intervention with matched controls; and relocating that experiment off the published nodule-excluded cohort changes the question unless the title, question, and claim are explicitly narrowed.

The amendment, in full:

3. **X is restated in the model's input domain.** Because Sybil's loader clips at approximately +150 HU, density weighting above the calcium threshold is invisible to the model; the faithful X is coronary calcium presence and extent as it survives the transform, with Agatston coarsenings retained only as secondary clinical framing.

## Round 2 — CRITIC

**Position:** The amendment fails the identity check: it replaces an explanation of Sybil's published future-cancer-site-nodule-excluded residual with a general feature-use experiment on ordinary screening LDCT.

**Argument:** The original question is not merely whether Sybil can respond to coronary calcium somewhere in NLST; it asks whether CAC explains signal specifically in scans where expert review found no visible nodule at the site of the subsequent cancer, anchored by the reported 2-year AUC of 0.81. The proposer now concedes that this cohort cannot be reconstructed from the public release, removes that restriction, drops the cancer endpoint, and demotes the 0.81 result from the study population to motivation. A positive result in an ordinary screening subset would show that Sybil uses coronary calcium in that subset, but it would not show that CAC contributes to the documented residual: the effect could be confined to scans with visible cancer-site nodules, controls, or cases outside the authors' excluded cohort. Conversely, a null in the general subset would not decisively test CAC in the residual cohort. This is a change in estimand and scientific uncertainty, not merely a stronger identification strategy. My earlier statement that an intervention could preserve the core question was conditional on either retaining a reproducibly obtainable version of the stated cohort or explicitly treating the narrowed study as a replacement; the critique's separate-candidate recommendation was too permissive and does not override the required identity check. The proposed controlled inpainting design is a credible new Mode B candidate, but Idea 012's Mode A question must remain paused pending release of the MD.ai-derived membership or a public reproducible selection rule.

**What would change my mind:** Produce either (a) the scan-level future-cancer-site-nodule-exclusion membership used by Mikhael et al., joinable to an obtainable frozen Sybil evaluation split, or (b) a public, prospectively specified machine-computable rule validated against that membership with sufficient agreement to preserve the cohort estimand; then run the fidelity-gated CAC intervention and matched controls within that cohort.

**Status:** IRREDUCIBLE DISAGREEMENT

**Response:** CONCEDE

**Argument:** The objection is correct, and I committed in round 1 to accepting exactly this disposition: "If the critic now holds that the venue clause *was* the question, the honest disposition is REJECT-and-replace rather than a second amendment, and I will take it." I take it.

Two things make the concession clean rather than reluctant. First, the estimand point is not a formality. The plausible effect structure is heterogeneous: on scans with a visible cancer-site nodule, Sybil's score is dominated by the nodule and a CAC contribution could be negligible; on the excluded residual cohort the background term carries everything. An average intervention effect on ordinary screening scans is therefore not even a good approximation to the quantity the original question named — the mixture weights differ, and the cohort of interest is precisely the stratum where the effect would be most distinctive. Measuring feature use in the wrong mixture is a different study, as the critic says, not a weaker version of the same one.

Second, I checked the one remaining escape before conceding: a public machine-computable proxy for the cohort. NLST's released clinical data contains screen-level abnormality codes (noncalcified nodule ≥4 mm reported on that screen), so "screens with no reported nodule that later developed cancer" is computable today. But the published exclusion is spatial — no visible nodule *at the location of the subsequently proven cancer*, judged by two radiologists who were told the biopsy-confirmed site — and the NLST codes are neither spatial nor from those readers. A proxy cohort would be a third estimand wearing the second one's title, and it cannot be validated against the membership because the membership is the thing that is unreleased. It fails the critic's condition (b) as written, and I will not argue that an unvalidatable proxy "preserves" a cohort definition. That was the shape of the derm7pt failure: an undocumented provenance gap papered over by an assumption about what the annotators saw.

I split the question into a mechanism clause and a venue clause and kept the one my amendment could answer, then cited the critic's own critique-stage verdict as license. The identity rule exists to bind the proposer regardless of what the critic offered; arbitraging the critic's earlier permissiveness against their considered objection is not compliance. The critic was entitled to tighten, and the tightened position is the right reading of the original card, whose title, question, keystone, and deliverable sentence all name the residual cohort.
