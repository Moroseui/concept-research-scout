# Novelty audit — scouting cycle 014

## C1 — The fat around the heart inside the CVD-mortality score: finish the observation Chao et al. started (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Chao et al., *Deep Learning Predicts Cardiovascular Disease Risks from Lung Cancer Screening Low Dose Computed Tomography*, Nature Communications 2021, DOI **10.1038/s41467-021-23235-4** (arXiv **2008.06997**): built Tri2D-Net and explicitly interpreted a high-risk case as attention to abundant juxtacardiac fat, concluding that both CAC and juxtacardiac fat contributed. (ii) Foldyna et al., *Automated epicardial adipose tissue quantification and cardiovascular risk prediction from lung cancer screening CT*, Communications Medicine 2024, DOI **10.1038/s43856-024-00475-1**, PMID **38480863**: validated automated EAT measurement on NLST LDCT and tested its outcome association. (iii) Zeleznik et al., *Deep convolutional neural networks to predict cardiovascular risk from computed tomography*, Nature Communications 2021, DOI **10.1038/s41467-021-20966-2**: automated CAC on chest CT and evaluated cardiovascular risk in NLST.
2. **Delta.** Quantitatively estimates whether EAT explains Tri2D-Net score variation conditional on CAC and rival within-crop measurements; this is a useful decomposition, but it strengthens an interpretation Tri2D-Net's authors already stated rather than discovering a new candidate feature.
3. **Why not done — BLIND_SPOT.** The model paper treated attention examples as sufficient interpretation, while the later EAT paper optimized biomarker prognosis rather than model decoding; the missing conditional score decomposition sits between explainable-AI and opportunistic-biomarker incentives.
4. **Verdict — INCREMENTAL.** The exact multivariable decomposition was not found, but “the model uses juxtacardiac fat” is already an explicit claim in the anchor paper, so the proposed physician-facing sentence is not novel.

## C2 — Redraw the same airway walls with a sharper pencil: does the peribronchial-thickening score follow Pi10? (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Hamamci et al., *CT-CLIP: Clinical Language-Image Pre-training for 3D Chest CT Volume*, arXiv **2403.17834**: introduced CT-CLIP/CT-RATE and the peribronchial-thickening output, without physical-cue decoding. (ii) Abadi et al., *Development and Application of a Virtual Imaging Trial framework for Airway Quantifications via CT*, 2023, PMCID **PMC10142146**: measured Pi10/WA% under scanner, kernel, matrix, and slice-thickness changes against known phantom geometry. (iii) Mets et al., *Follow-up of CT-derived airway wall thickness: Correcting for changes in inspiration level improves reliability*, European Journal of Radiology 2016, PMID **27776653**: quantified repeat-scan Pi10 variability and showed lung-volume correction improves reliability.
2. **Delta.** Uses geometry-matched reconstruction pairs as a natural intervention and asks whether a named VLM output co-moves dose-responsively with independently measured Pi10; none of the neighbors connects reconstruction-induced Pi10 changes to a model score.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** The combination of CT-CLIP's released named head and CT-RATE's now-identified same-acquisition multi-reconstruction pairs makes a label-free score-versus-physical-measurement experiment possible.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE.** Searches spanning CT foundation-model interpretation, Pi10 reconstruction sensitivity, virtual trials, and repeat CT found both halves but no study joining them; the claim remains conditional on airway-tool viability and global-kernel-response controls.

## C3 — The cardiomegaly head may be reading the rib cage: Haller index beyond heart volume (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Hamamci et al., *CT-CLIP: Clinical Language-Image Pre-training for 3D Chest CT Volume*, arXiv **2403.17834**: introduced the CT-CLIP cardiomegaly head without decomposing its anatomical cues. (ii) Haller et al., *Use of CT scans in selection of patients for pectus excavatum surgery: a preliminary report*, Journal of Pediatric Surgery 1987, DOI **10.1016/S0022-3468(87)80585-7**, PMID **3681619**: introduced the transverse-to-AP thoracic index used to quantify pectus severity. (iii) Wang et al., *Establishment and Reliability of an Automatic Measurement Method of Pectus Excavatum Indices Using a Deep Learning Model*, 2025, PMCID **PMC12204242**: automated Haller, correction, and asymmetry indices from CT and compared them with observers.
2. **Delta.** Tests whether a pretrained cardiomegaly score contains a Haller-index gradient after conditioning on measured heart volume; prior work measures pectus or predicts cardiomegaly, but the search found no decoding study connecting the two.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** A released 3D report-language model with a cardiomegaly output plus reliable automated whole-volume anatomy segmentation makes population-scale conditional decomposition inexpensive.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH.** No exact duplicate was found, but the search was limited by sparse indexing around model-error mechanisms and did not verify the card's stronger premise that clinicians systematically over-call cardiomegaly in high-Haller chests.

## C4 — Sybil was never given the patient's age; the thymus wrote it down anyway (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Mikhael et al., *Sybil: A Validated Deep Learning Model to Predict Future Lung Cancer Risk From a Single Low-Dose Chest Computed Tomography*, Journal of Clinical Oncology 2023, DOI **10.1200/JCO.22.01345**: built Sybil and evaluated age-stratified performance, without decoding an image-derived age cue. (ii) Lu et al., *Thymic health consequences in adults*, Nature 2026, DOI **10.1038/s41586-026-10242-y**, PMID **41851466**: built an automatic CT thymic-health score, applied it to 25,031 NLST participants, and associated it with lung-cancer incidence independently of age, sex, smoking, and comorbidities. (iii) Araki et al., *Normal thymus in adults: appearance on CT and associations with age, sex, BMI and smoking*, European Radiology 2016, DOI **10.1007/s00330-015-3796-y**, PMID **25925358**: quantified adult thymic attenuation and morphology and their demographic associations.
2. **Delta.** The only remaining distinct test is whether Sybil's own predictions depend on the already-established automated thymic-health signal; the proposed custom thymic-bed measurement and NLST risk association are substantially pre-empted by Lu et al.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** Sybil's released model permits a cross-model score decomposition that Lu et al. did not perform, although the thymus measurement capability itself is no longer new.
4. **Verdict — INCREMENTAL.** Not an exact duplicate because Lu et al. did not interrogate Sybil, but a 2026 primary paper has already operationalized the thymic signal on the same cohort and endpoint family; the card must be reframed as a narrow Sybil-dependence test and cite/use that measurement.

## C5 — Chronic anemia turns the marrow back on: is Merlin reading red marrow, not just pale blood? (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Blankemeier et al., *Merlin: a computed tomography vision-language foundation model and dataset*, Nature 2026, DOI **10.1038/s41586-026-10181-8**: released phenotype-classification inference for an abdominal CT foundation model, without physical-feature decoding of anemia. (ii) Koyama et al., *Investigation of factors affecting CT attenuation and glucose metabolism of bone marrow as seen on PET/CT scans*, 2024, PMCID **PMC10944371**: analyzed marrow CT number and notes elevated marrow attenuation in anemia/hematopoietic activation. (iii) Kamel et al., *Radiological profile of anemia on unenhanced MDCT of the thorax*, European Radiology 2008, DOI **10.1007/s00330-008-0950-9**, PMID **18386013**: showed aortic attenuation correlates with hemoglobin and evaluated the aortic-ring/septum signs.
2. **Delta.** Compares two spatially distinct physical anemia cues inside one foundation-model score—vertebral marrow attenuation conditional on blood-pool attenuation—with a prespecified sign; no neighbor tests which cue a CT model uses.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** Merlin's newly released phenotype-classification interface makes per-phenotype score decoding possible, conditional on the unverified existence and exposure of an anemia output.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH.** No duplicate was found across Merlin, CT anemia, and marrow-attenuation searches, but evidence that routine single-energy vertebral HU identifies red-marrow reconversion is thin and the model-head keystone is uninspected; this is a novelty-positive but feasibility-fragile result.
