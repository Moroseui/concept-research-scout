# Novelty audit — scouting cycle 010

## C1 — CXR-Age put back together from parts a radiologist can measure (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Raghu et al., “Deep Learning to Estimate Biological Age From Chest Radiographs,” *JACC: Cardiovascular Imaging* (2021), DOI `10.1016/j.jcmg.2021.01.008`: introduced CXR-Age and related its age estimate to mortality, but did not decompose the released model into geometric measurements. (ii) Chang et al., “Explainable Biological Age from Automated Chest Radiography–based Organ Quantifications: qCXR-bioage,” *Radiology: Cardiothoracic Imaging* (2026), DOI `10.1148/ryct.250327`: constructed a new transparent biological-age model from lung area, heart area, aortic diameter, bone density, and other automated measurements. (iii) Kallianos et al., “External Testing of a Deep Learning Model to Estimate Biologic Age Using Chest Radiographs,” *Radiology: Artificial Intelligence* (2024), DOI `10.1148/ryai.230433`: externally tested CXR-Age’s prognostic behavior, without testing which measurable anatomy drives it.
2. **Delta.** The candidate interrogates the released CXR-Age checkpoint itself and asks whether CTR and projected lung area account for its output; qCXR-bioage instead proves that related measurements can build a different age model.
3. **Why not done — `NEW_CAPABILITY`.** CheXmask now provides downloadable heart/lung masks aligned to several public CXR collections, while the original CXR-Age weights are public; this makes checkpoint-level decomposition possible without a new annotation campaign.
4. **Verdict — `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE`.** Searches spanning the original model, its external validation, explainable biological-age models, and explicit CTR/lung-area terms found close neighbors but no study decomposing the released CXR-Age output as proposed.

## C2 — Atelectasis vs consolidation: has CT-CLIP learned the radiologist's volume-loss rule? (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Hamamci et al., “Developing Generalist Foundation Models from a Multimodal Dataset for 3D Computed Tomography,” arXiv:`2403.17834`: introduced CT-RATE and CT-CLIP and evaluated abnormality outputs, without a lobar-volume mechanism analysis. (ii) Bankier et al., “Fleischner Society: Glossary of Terms for Thoracic Imaging,” *Radiology* (2024), DOI `10.1148/radiol.232558`: defines atelectasis by reduced volume/higher attenuation and associated fissural, bronchovascular, or mediastinal displacement; it establishes the candidate X but does not study a model. (iii) “Deep learning-based quantification of lobar volume loss in postoperative atelectasis,” *npj Digital Medicine* (2026), DOI `10.1038/s41746-026-02683-6`: developed automated lobar segmentation/volumetry for atelectasis and distinguished volume loss from consolidation-like opacity, without connecting the measurement to CT-CLIP outputs.
2. **Delta.** The candidate relates the within-scan CT-CLIP atelectasis-minus-consolidation score contrast to automated lobar volume loss conditional on opacity burden; none of the neighbors tests that model-specific relationship.
3. **Why not done — `NEW_CAPABILITY`.** The recent combination of a released 3D report-supervised model with separate atelectasis/consolidation outputs and scalable lobe volumetry makes this audit practical.
4. **Verdict — `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH`.** No duplicate was found, but the 2026 lobar-volumetry article was inspected only through its abstract/search-accessible material, and broad interpretability terminology may hide related work. Novelty does not cure the design issue that conditional association alone is weaker than the proposed “uses” sentence.

## C3 — Merlin's cirrhosis signal may be the spleen (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Blankemeier et al., “Merlin: a computed tomography vision–language foundation model and dataset,” *Nature* (2026; preprint arXiv:`2406.06512`): introduced the abdominal CT model and phenotype tasks, but did not attribute a liver-disease output to spleen volume. (ii) Pickhardt et al., “Fully Automated and Explainable Liver Segmental Volume Ratio and Spleen Segmentation at CT for Diagnosing Cirrhosis,” *Radiology: Artificial Intelligence* (2022), DOI `10.1148/ryai.210268`: built an explainable cirrhosis classifier from automated liver and spleen volumes; this is extremely close in measured vocabulary but is not a decomposition of Merlin. (iii) “CT Rule-in and Rule-out Criteria for Clinically Significant Portal Hypertension in Chronic Liver Disease,” *Radiology* (2024), DOI `10.1148/radiol.231208`: evaluated automated spleen volume and other CT criteria against clinically significant portal hypertension, establishing spleen volume as a relevant measurable marker rather than a Merlin cue.
2. **Delta.** The candidate asks whether an already-trained generalist foundation model’s liver-disease score follows spleen volume, including non-hepatic splenomegaly dissociation cases; prior work builds or validates explicit spleen/liver biomarkers instead.
3. **Why not done — `NEW_CAPABILITY`.** Merlin’s newly released abdominal CT weights and phenotype framework create a specific black box to decode with existing automated organ segmentation.
4. **Verdict — `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE`.** Model-name, cirrhosis, splenomegaly, spleen-volume, explainability, and automated-CT searches found a very close transparent classifier but no Merlin attribution study. The novelty claim remains conditional on confirming that a runnable liver-disease output exists.

## C4 — The inferior vena cava as a manometer: does the chest model read venous pressure? (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Hamamci et al., “Developing Generalist Foundation Models from a Multimodal Dataset for 3D Computed Tomography,” arXiv:`2403.17834`: introduced CT-CLIP’s chest-abnormality outputs, without analyzing IVC geometry. (ii) Matsumoto et al., “Estimating right atrial pressure using upright computed tomography in patients with heart failure,” *Scientific Reports* (2023), DOI `10.1038/s41598-023-34554-7`: compared CT-derived IVC area with catheter-measured right-atrial pressure, establishing the CT measurement but not learned-model use. (iii) Griffin et al., “Inferior Vena Cava Diameter Measurement Provides Distinct and Complementary Information to Right Atrial Pressure in Acute Decompensated Heart Failure,” *Journal of Cardiac Failure* (2022), DOI `10.1016/j.cardfail.2022.02.014`: found only modest IVC-diameter/RAP correlation and showed IVC change may reflect volume information distinct from pressure.
2. **Delta.** The candidate tests whether longitudinal changes in CT-CLIP cardiomegaly/effusion scores track CT IVC area and roundness after measured fluid, heart size, and lung volume are held constant; the neighbors validate either the model or the physiology, never their connection.
3. **Why not done — `BLIND_SPOT`.** Chest-model interpretability is organized around named thoracic findings, while IVC mechanics sits across radiology, echocardiography, and heart-failure physiology; the candidate connects those disciplinary frames.
4. **Verdict — `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH`.** No model–IVC duplicate appeared, but terminology varies across congestion, volume status, RAP, and vena-caval indices. The physiology literature also warns that “central-venous-pressure gauge” is too specific: a positive may identify venous-congestion/volume geometry rather than pressure itself.

## C5 — Aortic tortuosity as a buckled column: is the hypertension head reading exceeded critical pressure? (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Blankemeier et al., “Merlin: a computed tomography vision–language foundation model and dataset,” *Nature* (2026; preprint arXiv:`2406.06512`): includes five-year hypertension prediction but no tortuosity attribution. (ii) Han, “A biomechanical model of artery buckling,” *Journal of Biomechanics* (2007), DOI `10.1016/j.jbiomech.2007.06.018`: derived critical buckling pressure as a function of vessel geometry, stiffness, and axial strain and linked buckling to tortuosity. (iii) Han et al., “Artery buckling: new phenotypes, models, and applications,” *Annals of Biomedical Engineering* (2013), DOI `10.1007/s10439-012-0707-0`, PMID `23192265`: reviewed experimental and theoretical artery buckling and its associations with pressure, aging, tethering, and wall properties.
2. **Delta.** The candidate asks whether Merlin’s hypertension output tracks automated abdominal-aortic tortuosity and exploratory buckling signatures; the neighbors separately provide the prediction model and the biomechanical hypothesis.
3. **Why not done — `NEW_CAPABILITY`.** Merlin’s released five-year disease-prediction checkpoint supplies a newly inspectable hypertension score on volumetric CT, while off-the-shelf aortic segmentation makes population-scale centerline analysis feasible.
4. **Verdict — `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH`.** No direct Merlin/tortuosity attribution study was found. However, a score–tortuosity association, even with age and calcium adjustment, cannot verify that “critical pressure has been exceeded”: stiffness, axial tethering, wall weakening, and aging are co-determinants in the primary buckling theory. Novelty is plausible; the mechanistic deliverable is presently overidentified.
