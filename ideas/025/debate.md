# Debate transcript

## Round 1 — CRITIC

**Position:** The proposed repairs still cannot identify the original claim that the model uses patient movement or “restlessness,” because the released series contain only the output of a closed co-registration/resampling pipeline and no independent record of the movement that produced it.

**Argument:** Bone-anchored registration, off-bolus inconsistency energy, synthetic recovery tests, and a no-motion kinetic null can make an image statistic less sensitive to bolus passage, but they validate detection of residual spatial inconsistency—not its attribution to patient behavior. In the released ISLES'24 data, icobrain cva has already co-registered and temporally resampled the CTP (Riedel et al., DOI 10.1148/ryai.250603); the original frames, correction transforms, motion grades, and correction implementation are unavailable. Therefore a high “motion” index can still be caused by correction interpolation, intra-rotation artifacts, anatomy-dependent registration error, or scanner behavior, and a low index can reflect successful correction despite substantial original movement. The proposed injected-then-recorrected arm does not close this gap: applying a different rigid correction to already corrected 1-Hz images cannot recreate the proprietary pipeline's mapping from real acquisition motion to released residue. Even a discriminator that cannot separate synthetic from natural high-index images shows only indistinguishability under that discriminator, not shared causal origin. This is an identifiability failure at X itself, before asking whether the model uses X. The identity check therefore matters: narrowing the sentence to “the model uses a post-correction temporal-inconsistency index” would be testable, but it would no longer answer the original behavioral-actigraphy question and must be a successor candidate under the claim-identity rule. Stage 0 is worthwhile as a QC census, but it cannot be called deliverable-bearing for the present sentence.

**What would change my mind:** Show a paired validation set from the same or demonstrably matched CTP pipeline containing uncorrected acquisitions plus corrected outputs and independent motion ground truth (retained rigid transforms, external tracking, or a validated acquisition-level motion estimate), and prespecify that the bone/off-bolus index recovers patient-level motion rank after correction with useful accuracy; additionally show that the available injection/correction procedure reproduces the measured real-motion-to-residue relationship. Without that bridge, retain the original idea only as paused, or register the index-level robustness audit as a separate candidate.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 1 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** The released ISLES'24 series are the output of a closed correction pipeline with no independent record of the movement that produced them; therefore a post-correction inconsistency index cannot be attributed to patient behavior (high values have non-behavioral causes, low values may hide successfully corrected movement), the injection arm cannot reproduce the proprietary motion-to-residue mapping, and narrowing the sentence to the index level would change the claim's identity. X fails identifiability before the use question is even posed.

**Response:** AMEND — the attribution gap is real and the card as critiqued does not close it; I adopt the critic's own bridge, in the only obtainable form, as a mandatory gate. Two components of the objection are first rebutted because they over-count the alternatives; the amendment then addresses the one that survives.

**Argument:**

*Bounded rebuttal 1 — the alternative-cause list conflates mediators with confounds.* Correction interpolation and intra-rotation artifacts are not alternatives to patient movement; they are causally downstream of it. A correction pipeline interpolates and warps where motion drove it to; intra-rotation inconsistency is produced by movement during the rotation. Residue of those kinds *is* the motion signature, transformed — attributing it to "the pipeline" rather than the patient is like attributing a seismogram to the pen. On a bone-anchored index, the genuinely non-behavioral parents of residual displacement reduce to three: (i) the organizers' intensity-driven rigid co-registration being misled by bolus kinetics (a rigid error moves bone too — this is the live confound, and it correlates with severity through kinetics; conceded); (ii) scanner/table behavior (stratifiable by site and scanner metadata); (iii) my instrument's noise floor (bounded by the recovery and null-instrument checks already specified in the critique). The identifiability problem is concentrated in (i), not spread over an open-ended list.

*Bounded rebuttal 2 — "low index despite real movement" is attenuation, not misattribution.* Successful correction of a restless patient biases the L1 correlation toward null and shrinks the census dynamic range — which is already a preregistered pause gate. It cannot manufacture a false-positive attribution. And the within-case injection arm never required baseline cases to be truly motion-free, only that added dose moves the index, which the recovery check verifies.

*The amendment — an external validation bridge, added as a mandatory gate before any ISLES'24 model work (Stage 0b).* The critic's "what would change my mind" asks for uncorrected acquisitions plus corrected outputs with acquisition-level motion ground truth. Same-pipeline is unattainable — icobrain cva is closed — but the corpus for a pipeline-robustness version exists and was verified this session: **UniToBrain** (arXiv 2208.00650 [fetched, full text read this session]; dataset DOI 10.21227/x8ea-vh16; 258 patients, open access) releases CTP as raw DICOM time series (89 volumes over 44.5 s in the homogeneous GE sub-sample), and its rigid motion correction is described only inside the ground-truth map-derivation pipeline ("A motion correction is required and was performed using a rigid registration method"), not as applied to the released scans ("The raw pre-processing inputs are a collection of multiple CT scans and multiple targets maps for each patient, in DICOM format"). That the released frames are uncorrected is a source-supported interpretation, not yet an inspected fact — confirming it by opening one case's DICOMs is the first gate of Stage 0b. The bridge then: (a) measure acquisition-level motion on the uncorrected frames by bone-anchored registration — on uncorrected data this is a validated motion estimate, the same class of measurement PMID 34678666 used on acquisitions; (b) pass the same series through several independent rigid correction implementations plus 1-Hz temporal resampling, mimicking the released ISLES'24 description; (c) prespecify a Spearman threshold: the post-correction bone/off-bolus index must recover patient-level original-motion rank under *every* tested pipeline; (d) characterize each pipeline's kinetic false-residue on low-motion, kinetics-varied cases — directly bounding confound (i); (e) require injected-then-recorrected residue to reproduce the measured real-motion-to-residue relationship within a stated tolerance. If the bridge holds across all tested pipelines, the surviving escape hypothesis is "icobrain cva is the exceptional pipeline that erases or inverts a mapping every tested implementation preserves" — carried explicitly as a limitation, not silently. If the bridge fails — rank recovery not robust, or UniToBrain turns out to be corrected after all and no substitute corpus is found — the behavioral sentence is unreachable with released data and the idea **PAUSES** (revisit trigger: organizers release transforms or uncorrected series), with the index-level robustness audit recorded as a spin-off successor candidate, not adopted in place.

*Secondary attribution fingerprint, preregistered:* PMID 34678666 gives a three-covariate signature of real patient motion in stroke CTP — more motion with higher NIHSS, older age, and *shorter onset-to-scan time*. The scanner/table pathway predicts no onset-to-scan dependence; the kinetic-confounding pathway has mechanisms for NIHSS and age (cardiac output, dispersed boluses) but none for onset-to-scan time. Reproducing the full signature in the ISLES'24 census is converging evidence for behavioral attribution, weighted as such, not proof.

*Identity check:* the deliverable sentence is unchanged — the amendment adds a validation study and gates, and defines PAUSE, not narrowing, as the failure outcome. What was lost, stated plainly: the study is no longer self-contained in ISLES'24 (a second corpus and a multi-pipeline registration workstream now precede any model work, roughly doubling Stage 0 cost); even in success, behavioral attribution rests on a stated pipeline-robustness assumption rather than same-pipeline ground truth; and I concede the critic's final point — the ISLES'24 census alone is a gate and a reusable QC covariate, not deliverable-bearing for the behavioral sentence until the bridge passes.

**Status:** OPEN — the critic should judge whether multi-pipeline rank recovery plus residue matching on a verified uncorrected public corpus is an acceptable substitute for the unattainable same-pipeline ground truth, and whether the UniToBrain uncorrected-status inspection is the right first gate.

## Round 2 — CRITIC

**Position:** The UniToBrain amendment preserves the original question, but it does not supply the independent motion ground truth or matched correction pipeline needed to identify an ISLES'24 residual as patient behavior.

**Argument:** The identity check passes: the proposed study still asks whether a raw-CTP model uses residual patient movement, and it explicitly pauses rather than silently substituting an index-level claim if the bridge fails. The new bridge nevertheless fails at its load-bearing step. Its “acquisition-level motion” reference is itself bone-anchored rigid registration of the uncorrected images—the same class of image-derived estimator whose construct validity is at issue—not retained scanner transforms, external tracking, or independently validated motion labels. Agreement between that estimator before correction and a related bone/off-bolus statistic after correction can therefore arise from shared anatomy-dependent registration error, table motion, or reconstruction artifacts; it is not independent validation of patient movement. More importantly, testing several investigator-chosen rigid pipelines cannot bound the behavior of icobrain cva 1.5.0, because that closed pipeline is the treatment that created the ISLES'24 residue. “Every tested implementation preserves rank” does not make “the untested implementation preserves rank” a negligible escape hypothesis, especially when acquisition cadence, scanner platform, reconstruction, and temporal resampling may differ between UniToBrain and ISLES'24. The proposed NIHSS/age/onset fingerprint is converging association only: onset-to-scan time can covary with referral pathway, workflow, treatment selection, stroke subtype, and acquisition site, so it cannot repair the missing same-pipeline causal bridge. I therefore do not accept the amendment as answering the round-1 objection. This is now an irreducible evidence gap, not a design detail that another sham or threshold can settle.

**What would change my mind:** Produce data processed by icobrain cva 1.5.0—or a source-verified bit-equivalent pipeline—with paired pre-correction CTP and released-style corrected output plus motion ground truth independent of image registration (scanner/correction transforms validated against external tracking, or blinded acquisition-level motion grades validated for rank recovery), and show that the prespecified post-correction index recovers patient-motion rank and that the injection procedure reproduces the real motion-to-residue mapping. Alternatively, organizer confirmation plus retained transforms for a representative ISLES'24 subset would settle the same issue. Without one of these, pause the behavioral candidate and register the post-correction inconsistency audit as a separate successor.

**Status:** IRREDUCIBLE DISAGREEMENT

## Round 2 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** The bridge fails at its load-bearing step: the "acquisition-level motion" reference is itself image-derived rigid registration, not independent ground truth; and no amount of multi-pipeline robustness testing can bound icobrain cva 1.5.0, because that closed pipeline is the very treatment that produced the ISLES'24 residue. The fingerprint is association only. The gap is an evidence gap, not a design detail.

**Response:** CONCEDE

**Argument:** The critic is right on the point that decides the outcome, and I will not spend a third round defending a bridge that cannot reach the far bank.

One bounded clarification first, for the record and for the successor — it does not reverse the verdict. On component (a), skull-anchored rigid tracking of *uncorrected* frames is not "the same class of estimator whose validity is at issue." The disputed construct is a *post-correction* residue statistic; on uncorrected frames, displacement of a rigid, contrast-invariant skull across a ~1-minute series *is* head-in-scanner motion up to table travel (recorded in DICOM and stratifiable) and the registration noise floor (boundable by synthetic recovery). It is the same instrument class Fahmi 2013 and PMID 34678666 used as their motion measurement, and the fMRI FD literature the card borrowed from has never had external tracking either. If the field's acquisition-level instrument is inadmissible as ground truth, those anchor papers dissolve too. So the bridge does not fail at step (a).

It fails at step (b), and that failure is sufficient. Icobrain cva 1.5.0 is closed, its transforms were not retained in the release, and it is the treatment that generated the object of study. "Every tested open implementation preserves motion rank" is evidence about a family the treatment may not belong to — and the critic is right that cadence, platform, reconstruction, and resampling differences between UniToBrain and ISLES'24 widen that gap rather than narrow it. My round-1 amendment already named this as the surviving escape hypothesis to be "carried explicitly as a limitation"; the critic's reply exposes what that phrasing conceded implicitly: a limitation that cannot be bounded in principle with obtainable data is not a limitation, it is the answer. A behavioral-attribution claim resting on it would be exactly the fluent-nonsense failure the charter warns against — a sentence a radiologist could read, built on an attribution no obtainable measurement can defend. And I concede the fingerprint point as argued: onset-to-scan time covaries with referral pathway and workflow, so the three-covariate signature is converging association, not a causal bridge.

Outcome, per my own round-1 failure clause, which this situation triggers: the behavioral candidate **PAUSES**. Revisit trigger, taken directly from the critic's settling conditions: organizer or icometrix correspondence yielding retained correction transforms, paired pre-correction series for a representative ISLES'24 subset, or a source-verified equivalent pipeline with motion ground truth independent of the disputed estimator class.

**Spin-off** (successor candidate under the 2026-08-10 claim-identity rule, parent idea-025, to re-enter through normal scouting — not adopted in place): the post-correction temporal-inconsistency audit. Deliverable sentence at the index level: *the model is using a bolus-independent temporal-inconsistency signature in the released series* — X computable today, no attribution to patient behavior claimed. The bolus-robust instrument repairs from critique §3 (bone-anchored index, off-bolus energy, null-instrument and recovery checks) transfer to it intact, as does the Stage-0 census, which retains standalone value as the keystone inspection and as a per-case QC covariate for ideas 021 and 023 on the same dataset — value both sides have already endorsed.

What was lost, stated plainly: the actigraphy reading — motion as behavior, the named sedation/intubation harm, the L1 severity link — is unreachable with released data, and the successor keeps only the measurable shadow of it.

**Status:** CONCEDED
