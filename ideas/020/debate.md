# Debate transcript

## Round 1 — CRITIC

**Position:** The proposed topology swap cannot presently identify use of perfusion-front topology, because changing connectivity at fixed threshold volumes necessarily introduces model-visible non-topological changes whose adequacy cannot be certified by the proposed discriminator.

**Argument:** A Tmax>10 component can be detached or a bridge severed only by changing voxel values along some cut set; preserving the global histogram, threshold volumes, and Fourier power spectrum does not preserve local gradients, phase, vascular-territory plausibility, or deconvolution texture. An ISLES'24 infarct model could respond to any of those changes without representing connected-component count, Euler characteristic, curvature, or core contact. A near-chance real-versus-edit discriminator is only a failed detection test, not evidence of equivalence, especially with roughly 30 cases, and topology-preserving shams do not isolate topology unless their local perturbation geometry is exchangeable with the topology-changing edits. Thus even the stronger proposed readout in an untouched island would show nonlocal sensitivity to the edit, not specifically sensitivity to topology. This repeats the live identifiability problem in ideas 008 and 011, and the verified OOD lesson from idea 006; histogram and spectrum matching do not yet make it different in the load-bearing respect. The Stage-0 observational screen may establish incremental prognostic association, but it cannot repair this model-use inference and therefore cannot by itself support the original deliverable sentence. The identity check passes only if Stage 0 remains a gate rather than becoming the endpoint.

**What would change my mind:** Show, before model testing, a prespecified synthetic-field validation in which topology-changing and topology-preserving edits are matched not only on histogram and spectrum but also on the distributions of every changed voxel's local value/gradient patch and vascular-territory location; then demonstrate with adequate equivalence margins that multiple independently trained edit detectors cannot distinguish the two edit classes, and show that a bank of topology-blind local and global-summary control models has zero differential response while a topology-aware positive-control model is detected at a prespecified minimum effect. If those controls pass on held-out ISLES maps, the intervention would have evidence that it isolates topology rather than merely edit class.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 1 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** Any edit that changes connectivity must change voxel values along some cut set, so every topology-altering edit carries non-topological, model-visible side effects — local gradients, phase, territory plausibility, deconvolution texture — that global histogram/spectrum matching does not constrain. A near-chance discriminator on ~30 cases is a failed detection test, not an equivalence certificate; and the untouched-island readout, by itself, demonstrates only that the model is nonlocal, not that it reads topology, unless the topology-changing and topology-preserving arms are exchangeable in their local perturbation geometry — which nothing in the current card guarantees. As written, this repeats the edit-validity failure that has ideas 006, 008, and 011 stuck.

**Response:** AMEND

**Argument:** The critic is right on the load-bearing point: the card's certification story (global summary matching plus a one-sided discriminator) cannot make the two edit arms comparable, and the island readout alone establishes nonlocality, not topology use. I will not defend that version. The amendment makes two changes.

**(1) Matched-cut construction — exchangeability by shared operator, not by discriminator faith.** Replace free-form topology swaps with a single edit operator applied identically in both arms: sever a thin Tmax>10 bridge using one fixed cut geometry (same width, same depth profile, same value-replacement rule drawn from the sub-threshold neighborhood). The arms differ only in the *global role* of the severed bridge. In the topology-changing arm, the bridge is the unique connector between two subregions, so the cut changes the component count. In the sham arm, an equivalent-calibre second connection exists elsewhere, so the identical local operation leaves topology unchanged. The local perturbation is then the same operator class in both arms by construction, and the primary endpoint is the *differential* response at an untouched probe region whose entire model-visible neighborhood is bit-identical across arms. A model that responds to cut texture, gradient disruption, or edit artifacts responds to them equally in both arms; only the connectivity consequence differs between arms. This is the specific respect in which the design now differs from ideas 008 and 011: those interventions substitute tissue or texture, so their validity question is the open-ended, one-sided "is this synthesis in-distribution?" Here the certification burden becomes the two-sample question "are the two arms' cut sites exchangeable?" — which is testable with stated power, because both arms' cut-site populations are constructed and observable. The residual threat the critic should press on is real and is now named as a gate: unique-bridge and redundant-bridge sites may occur in systematically different local contexts (unique bridges may be systematically thinner, more peripheral, or more artifact-prone). The design therefore requires a prespecified balance test on cut-site local morphology and value/gradient patch distributions between arms, with an equivalence margin, before any model inference; failure of balance kills the intervention arm.

**(2) The critic's battery is adopted as prespecified gates, in order, all before the frozen model is queried.** (a) Stage-0 observational topology screen on the 149 public cases (per the critique) as the first kill gate. (b) Synthetic smooth-field demonstration that the matched-cut construction is satisfiable at all. (c) Changed-voxel patch-distribution matching between arms with equivalence margins, verified by multiple independently trained edit detectors, not one. (d) Control-model bank on held-out maps: topology-blind local and global-summary models must show zero differential response at the probe region; a topology-aware positive control must show a detectable differential at a prespecified minimum effect. Failure at any gate parks the idea with the Stage-0 observational result only, which is recorded now as *not* supporting the deliverable sentence.

**What is lost, plainly.** First, the primary claim narrows from "fragmentation, curvature and contact with the core" to fragmentation/connectivity alone: only the bridge-cut operator admits the matched-arm construction directly, so curvature and core-contact drop to secondary status, each needing its own matched-operator design before any claim about them. Second, feasibility drops further: the modal outcome is now parking at a rung-0 observational association, and the timeline roughly doubles, because two validation gates precede the first model query. Third, the study acquires a new empirical support requirement — enough real maps must contain both unique-bridge and balance-matched redundant-bridge sites — which nobody has counted, and which becomes part of gate (b)/(c).

**Identity check.** The original one-sentence question is "is an ISLES'24 model using the topology of the perfusion-deficit boundary to distinguish tissue that will die from tissue that will recover?" Connectedness is the core of that topology family, not a substitute for it; the amended primary endpoint answers the original question on a narrowed enumeration, and the deliverable sentence survives as "the final-infarct model is using the connectedness of the severe-delay front, not only perfusion volumes." The question's identity is preserved; the enumeration inside the sentence shrinks. This is narrowing within the same deliverable, not a new study wearing the old title.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 2 — CRITIC

**Position:** The matched-cut amendment still confounds the connectivity consequence with the pre-existing global differences between maps that contain a unique bridge and maps that contain a redundant bridge.

**Argument:** The identity check passes: narrowing the original topology question to connectedness still asks whether the final-infarct model uses perfusion-front topology, and Stage 0 remains a gate rather than a replacement endpoint. The shared cut operator also answers the earlier objection about unequal local edit texture. It does not, however, make the treatment arms exchangeable. A unique-bridge field and a redundant-bridge field differ before either cut in at least the location and geometry of the second connection, and plausibly in component size, tortuosity, territory occupancy, distance relationships, and long-range intensity organization. Matching the cut-site patches cannot match those global fields; indeed, their global connectedness structure is how the arms are selected. A difference in the cut-induced response at an untouched probe region could therefore be an interaction between the cut and any pre-existing global morphology that distinguishes the two site populations, not the causal effect of changing component count. Multiple edit detectors and topology-blind control models only test the finite alternatives they encode; they cannot turn two structurally selected populations into a counterfactual pair. This is a narrower objection than Round 1: local edit fingerprints have been adequately addressed in principle, but the proposed between-map comparison still does not isolate the claimed mechanism. The problem is especially acute with 149 cases, because restricting to balance-matched unique and redundant bridges may leave little common support even if both site types exist.

**What would change my mind:** Construct a within-field factorial intervention in which the same baseline map, cut site, probe region, and cut operation are reused while only a remote redundant connection is toggled, yielding the four required states (connection present/absent × cut present/absent). Prespecify a difference-in-differences estimand that subtracts the remote-connection edit's own effect, include a topology-preserving remote-edit sham matched on its complete local patch distribution, and show adequate common support plus the already proposed topology-blind and topology-aware control-model results on held-out real maps. If such a factorial construction cannot pass its validity gates, the defensible conclusion is nonlocal morphology sensitivity, not use of connectedness.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 2 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** The matched-cut arms are selected *by* their global connectedness structure, so unique-bridge maps and redundant-bridge maps differ in pre-existing global morphology before any cut is made. A differential probe-region response could therefore be an interaction between the cut and that pre-existing morphology, not the causal effect of changing component count. Balance tests on cut-site patches and finite detector banks cannot turn two structurally selected populations into a counterfactual pair — and thin common support in 149 cases makes the between-map comparison worse, not better.

**Response:** AMEND

**Argument:** The selection confound is correct and I do not contest it. Exchangeability between arms was supposed to come from the shared operator plus balance testing, but the arms are defined by the very global structure whose influence must be excluded; no covariate matching can undo selection on the variable under study. The between-map matched-cut comparison is withdrawn as the primary design. The amendment adopts the critic's within-field factorial as the primary construction, and adds one piece the factorial itself still needs.

**(1) The factorial, concretely.** For each usable case: a baseline Tmax map whose severe-delay region contains a unique thin bridge U connecting a probe-side subregion P to the main body Q, a fixed cut operator A applied to U (round-1 specification unchanged), and a synthetic remote redundant connection B of equal calibre added between P and Q at a site far from both U and the probe region, with values drawn from the empirical bridge statistics of real maps. Four states of the *same* map: {∅, A, B, AB}. Connectedness of P: intact in ∅, B, and AB; broken only in A. The probe region inside P has a bit-identical model-visible neighborhood in all four states. Primary estimand: DiD = [f(A) − f(∅)] − [f(AB) − f(B)] at the probe region — the response to the identical cut when it does versus does not disconnect, within one anatomy, one cut site, one probe. Selection across maps no longer enters the contrast; every map is its own control.

**(2) What the factorial still assumes, named rather than inherited silently.** DiD identifies the cut × connection *interaction*. Reading that interaction as connectivity use requires additivity of the non-topological effects: a model that responds nonadditively to two remote edits — for instance through a nonlinear dependence on total suprathreshold volume, which the four states do not hold exactly constant (they differ by the masses of U and B) — could produce a nonzero interaction while computing nothing topological. The critic's remote-edit sham is therefore adopted and given a precise job: a negative-control factorial in which the toggled remote element is a mass-matched, calibre-matched blind spur that attaches to P alone and never alters connectedness in either cut state. Its interaction estimate must fall within a prespecified equivalence margin; if it does not, additivity fails and the primary DiD is declared uninterpretable, not weakly positive. This control also directly exercises the residual volume pathway, because it moves suprathreshold mass identically without moving topology.

**(3) Gate ordering, updated.** The round-1 gate battery stands with two changes: gate (b), the synthetic-field demonstration, now must also count common support in the real maps — cases admitting simultaneously a unique bridge, a remote connection site, a mass-matched spur site, and a probe region remote from all edits — against a prespecified minimum N fixed before counting; and gate (c)'s detector battery now includes the added-connection edit B, since B is a synthesis (its main effect is subtracted by the estimand, but a grossly out-of-distribution B could shift the model's response regime and poison the interaction; the detectors bound that).

**What is lost, plainly.** This is the second structural amendment. First, the identifying assumption has moved, not vanished: between-arm exchangeability is replaced by additivity of non-topological effects — testable via the negative-control factorial, but an assumption all the same, and one the round-1 design did not need to state. Second, the synthesis burden partially re-enters: round 1 only deleted values along a cut; the factorial must *add* a plausible bridge, which is exactly the class of edit that has ideas 008 and 011 stuck, mitigated here only by main-effect subtraction and the detector gate, not eliminated. Third, common support shrinks again — four simultaneous geometric requirements per case, uncounted, in a 149-case corpus; the modal outcome is now parking at the Stage-0 observational result, and the timeline grows a further gate. Fourth, per the debate rules' own arithmetic: a third structural objection of this kind should be read as evidence that the original design was wrong rather than repairable, and I state that in advance rather than after it happens.

**Identity check.** The one-sentence question is unchanged: is an ISLES'24 model using the topology of the perfusion-deficit boundary to distinguish tissue that will die from tissue that will recover? The factorial is a stronger identification strategy for the same narrowed estimand fixed in round 1 — connectedness of the severe-delay front — and the deliverable sentence from round 1 survives verbatim. No spin-off is introduced.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 3 — CRITIC

**Position:** The factorial identifies a cut-by-added-bridge interaction, not use of connectedness, because its negative-control spur does not reproduce the non-topological long-range relationship created by a bridge joining P to Q.

**Argument:** The identity check still passes at the level of intent: a valid within-field manipulation of whether P remains connected would answer the narrowed version of the original topology question. The proposed estimand, however, does not isolate that manipulation. B is not merely suprathreshold mass remote from the probe: it is an elongated structure whose two endpoints lie in P and Q, so adding it changes long-range intensity organization, skeleton length, path geometry, loop structure, and the spatial relationship between two already abnormal regions. The blind-spur control matches mass and calibre but has only one attachment and therefore cannot match those model-visible properties. A topology-blind model with global attention could respond nonadditively when two remote high-Tmax structures are spatially aligned or jointly present, yielding a nonzero `[f(A)-f(∅)]-[f(AB)-f(B)]` while never computing component count or whether P is connected to Q. A null spur interaction would not exclude that mechanism; it tests only one special failure of additivity. Detector gates likewise address detectability of synthesis, not whether the frozen model's interaction is caused by connectivity. This is now an intrinsic identification problem rather than the between-map selection problem conceded in round 2: the only physical difference that makes B redundant is also a collection of non-topological field relations unavailable to the spur. The defensible positive sentence would be “the model has a nonadditive response to a remote bridge and a cut,” which is rung 1 but does not satisfy the amended deliverable “the model is using connectedness.” Replacing the endpoint with that sentence would fail the identity check and require a separate candidate. Because the proposer has already supplied the strongest plausible within-field factorial and the remaining distinction cannot be settled by the proposed observable controls, this is an irreducible disagreement about claim identifiability, not another request for engineering polish.

**What would change my mind:** Show an intervention family in which many geometrically distinct P–Q bridges and nonconnecting controls are matched on all prespecified non-topological field relations, and demonstrate that the frozen model's interaction is invariant across bridge geometry but switches specifically with graph connectivity; alternatively, expose an architectural or mechanistic decomposition proving that the measured interaction is mediated by a connected-component representation rather than generic nonlocal attention. Until one of those facts is shown, the Stage-0 observational study may proceed on its own terms, but no factorial positive supports the connectedness-use sentence.

**Status:** IRREDUCIBLE DISAGREEMENT

## Round 3 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** The only physical fact that makes B redundant — a second suprathreshold path whose endpoints lie in P and Q — is itself a bundle of non-topological long-range field relations: elongation, endpoint placement, alignment, skeleton and path geometry, the joint presence of two remote abnormal structures. The mass-matched blind spur, with one attachment, cannot reproduce any of them. A topology-blind model with global attention could therefore yield a nonzero DiD while never computing whether P connects to Q, a null spur interaction would not exclude that mechanism, and the defensible positive sentence — "the model has a nonadditive response to a remote bridge and a cut" — is not the deliverable sentence.

**Response:** CONCEDE

**Argument:** The objection is correct, and I will not attempt a fourth structural design.

**The concession on the merits.** The spur control tests one specific failure of additivity — suprathreshold mass — but redundancy is not mass. It is constituted by exactly the long-range relations the critic enumerates: a control that matched all of them would have two attachments spanning P and Q, at which point it *is* a connector and the control has converged to the treatment. Within the factorial's own logic there is no observable control that separates "responds to connectivity" from "responds nonadditively to the joint configuration of two remote suprathreshold structures." I did consider the natural escape living inside the critic's what-would-change-my-mind: a gapped-bridge family — B versus B-with-a-few-subthreshold-gap-voxels, replicated over many bridge geometries, with dose–response in gap width, so that every continuous long-range relation converges between arms while graph connectivity switches discretely. I am not submitting it as an amendment, for two reasons. First, it is not engineering polish on the factorial: it discards the DiD estimand for an invariance/specificity claim, and it imports a new untested identifying assumption — a continuity bound stating that the model's non-topological sensitivity to a few remote voxel changes is small relative to the claimed connectivity effect — which would itself need a validation battery nobody has built. The identifying assumption would move a fourth time: discriminator faith (round 0), between-arm exchangeability (round 1), additivity (round 2), continuity (round 3). It has never been discharged, only relocated. That is the signature of a wrong design, and it is the same signature as ideas 006, 008, 011, and 014. Second, my round-2 statement binds me: I said in advance that a third structural objection of this kind should be read as evidence that the original design was wrong rather than repairable. This is the third.

**The deeper point, stated plainly.** At this grain the behavioral question may be ill-posed. In a voxel field, connectedness is not separate from long-range intensity organization; it is a particular function of it. "Uses connectedness" versus "generic nonlocal sensitivity that co-varies with connection" is at bottom partly a representational claim about the model's internals — which is why the critic's second escape route is mechanistic decomposition, not another intervention. Behavioral edits alone may be unable to settle the connectedness-use sentence for any model with global receptive fields. That honestly caps this design family, not just this instance of it.

**What stands after the concession.** The Stage-0 observational screen proceeds on its own terms, per the critic's explicit allowance and the critique's §4: frozen topology statistics on the 149 real maps, tested for incremental information about final infarct beyond threshold volumes, HIR, and distance covariates, with center-held-out replication and a prespecified sensitivity statement. Recorded now, before any result exists: a positive Stage 0 supports association only and does not support the deliverable sentence; the connectedness-use sentence currently has no defensible design behind it, so the idea parks at rung 0 regardless of Stage-0 outcome.

**Spin-off (recorded, not defended):** the gapped-bridge invariance family — many geometrically distinct closed versus gapped P–Q connectors, dose–response in gap width, a prespecified continuity bound as the named identifying assumption, with the critic's mechanistic-decomposition route as the alternative — is a different identification strategy for the same deliverable sentence. Under the 2026-08-10 claim-identity governance it should enter, if ever, as a successor candidate with parent_ids through a normal scouting cycle, conditional on (a) a positive Stage-0 association and (b) the portfolio first demonstrating certified in-distribution synthesis anywhere, since it inherits the full bridge-synthesis burden this debate did not resolve.

**Status:** CONCEDED
