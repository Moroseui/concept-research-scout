# Debate summary — idea 020

## Agreed

- The original counterfactual topology-swap design did not identify topology use: changing connectivity also changes local gradients, phase, territory plausibility, and other model-visible features, while a near-chance edit discriminator would not establish equivalence (round 1).
- The primary model-use readout would need to be nonlocal: predictions should be measured in a probe region whose model-visible neighborhood is identical across edited and unedited inputs. A response at edited voxels would establish only sensitivity to changed perfusion values (round 1).
- Narrowing the target from a family including fragmentation, curvature, and core contact to connectedness preserves the identity of the original topology-use question, although it loses the broader enumeration (rounds 1–3).
- A shared cut operator can address unequal local edit texture, but comparing naturally unique-bridge and redundant-bridge maps remains confounded by pre-existing global morphology and support selection (round 2).
- A within-map factorial removes that between-map selection problem, but identifies a cut-by-added-bridge interaction rather than connectedness use unless the bridge's other long-range field relations can be excluded (round 3).
- The mass- and calibre-matched blind-spur control cannot exclude generic nonadditive sensitivity to endpoint placement, alignment, skeleton/path geometry, or the joint configuration of remote suprathreshold structures (round 3).
- The proposed behavioral-edit sequence therefore does not support the sentence that the model uses connectedness. A defensible positive would say only that the model responds nonadditively to a remote bridge and cut, which is a materially different deliverable (round 3).
- The observational Stage 0 remains worth doing on its own terms: test whether prespecified real-map topology statistics add information about final infarct beyond threshold volumes, HIR, and distance covariates, with center-held-out replication and prespecified sensitivity. Both sides agree that even a positive result establishes association only, not model use (rounds 1 and 3).
- The intervention remains burdened by unverified in-distribution synthesis and potentially sparse geometric support in 149 cases. Data and deterministic topology measurements themselves are available and do not depend on annotation provenance for the primary model-use readout (rounds 1–3).

## Unresolved

### Can a future intervention family identify connectedness use?

- **Question:** Could many geometrically varied closed-versus-gapped connectors, or a mechanistic decomposition of the model, separate graph connectedness from generic nonlocal sensitivity?
- **Proposer's position:** A gapped-bridge invariance family is conceivable, but it is a new identification strategy with an unvalidated continuity assumption and should enter only as a successor after a positive Stage 0 and an independent demonstration of certified in-distribution synthesis.
- **Critic's position:** Evidence could settle the issue if responses were invariant across many bridge geometries and switched specifically with connectivity while nonconnecting controls matched prespecified non-topological relations, or if an internal mechanistic decomposition showed mediation by a connected-component representation.
- **What evidence would settle it:** A separately designed and validated intervention family satisfying those invariance and matching conditions, or architectural/mechanistic evidence isolating a connected-component representation. No such evidence was presented in this debate.

### Does the observational topology signal exist with adequate independent support?

- **Question:** At matched Tmax threshold volumes, do fragmentation and core-contact measures add information about final infarct beyond HIR and distance covariates, and replicate across the two centers?
- **Proposer's position:** This is a cheap mandatory Stage-0 gate; a sensitivity-qualified null should park the idea, while a positive motivates—but does not validate—a future model-use study.
- **Critic's position:** Stage 0 may proceed and can establish incremental prognostic association, but cannot repair or substitute for the failed model-use inference.
- **What evidence would settle it:** A prespecified analysis of the 149 released cases with frozen measurements, adequate sensitivity or precision, and center-held-out replication. This settles only the association question.

### Is there enough support for any bridge-based successor?

- **Question:** Do enough real maps contain usable unique bridges, remote connector sites, matched controls, and probe regions to support a powered intervention?
- **Proposer's position:** This must be counted before model queries and was never inspected.
- **Critic's position:** Restrictive matching in 149 cases may leave inadequate common support.
- **What evidence would settle it:** A metadata/image-only support audit against a prespecified minimum sample requirement. It would establish feasibility, not identifiability or edit validity.

## Positions that moved

- **Proposer, round 1:** Conceded that global histogram/spectrum matching, a one-sided discriminator, and an untouched-island readout did not isolate topology. This followed the critic's argument that connectivity edits necessarily carry unequal local, territorial, and deconvolution-related changes. The proposer replaced free-form topology swaps with locally matched bridge cuts and adopted staged validity/control-model gates.
- **Proposer, round 2:** Conceded that the matched-cut between-map design remained selected on pre-existing global connectedness structure. This followed the critic's argument that unique-bridge and redundant-bridge maps cannot be made counterfactual pairs through cut-site matching. The proposer replaced it with a within-field factorial and difference-in-differences estimand plus a blind-spur negative-control factorial.
- **Proposer, round 3:** Conceded that the factorial's spur control could not match the non-topological long-range relations created by a P–Q bridge, so the interaction did not identify connectedness use. This directly answered the critic's new argument about endpoint placement, path geometry, and generic global-attention interactions. The concession was earned, not UNEARNED.
- **Critic, round 2:** Accepted that the shared cut operator addressed the narrower local edit-texture objection and that the identity check passed, while maintaining the global selection objection.
- **Critic, round 3:** Accepted that a valid within-field connectivity manipulation would answer the narrowed question and that the factorial removed the prior between-map selection problem, while maintaining the intrinsic bridge-versus-connectivity objection.

## Amendments made

At round zero, the idea claimed that topology-swapped Tmax fields preserving histograms, threshold volumes, geography, and smoothness could test whether a final-infarct model used fragmentation, curvature, and core contact. Its proposed certification relied substantially on topology-preserving shams and a real-versus-edit discriminator.

Round 1 narrowed the claimed X to connectedness and replaced free-form swaps with matched cuts of unique versus redundant bridges, a nonlocal probe-region endpoint, equivalence tests on cut-site patches, multiple edit detectors, and topology-blind/topology-aware control models. Curvature and core contact were lost from the primary claim.

Round 2 withdrew the between-map comparison and introduced a within-map four-state factorial—baseline, cut, added redundant bridge, and both—with a difference-in-differences endpoint. It added a mass- and calibre-matched blind-spur factorial to test non-topological nonadditivity. This reintroduced the difficult requirement to synthesize a plausible bridge and further reduced likely common support.

Round 3 withdrew the factorial as evidence for connectedness use. The surviving work is only the Stage-0 observational association study. The original deliverable sentence has no defensible experiment behind it in this candidate; replacing it with “the model has a nonadditive response to a remote bridge and a cut” would change claim identity. A gapped-bridge invariance design was recorded only as a possible successor, not adopted as another amendment.

## Recommendation

**REJECT.** The debate converged after three substantive objections, not in a perfunctory round: the proposer ultimately agreed that the available behavioral interventions cannot distinguish connectedness use from generic nonlocal sensitivity to the same long-range intensity organization. The observational Stage 0 is scientifically worthwhile, but it studies incremental association rather than the candidate's model-use deliverable and should not keep this idea active under that claim.

The single most important thing for the human to inspect is the claim boundary: whether to authorize the Stage-0 observational screen as a deliberately rung-0 result or register it separately, while recording that no positive from it supports “the model uses connectedness.” A future successor should require a positive Stage 0 plus independently demonstrated, certified synthesis and an invariance or mechanistic test that isolates connectivity.
