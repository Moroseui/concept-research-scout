## OVERVIEW

This study cannot answer the feature question in either direction, and the premise behind it is not what the study found. The experiment did not show that CBV "doesn't matter biologically." It tested one specific, pre-registered way of relating a joint CBV/MTT coordinate to final tissue death, and that particular test failed because the relationship pointed in opposite directions across blood-flow bands instead of forming the stable pattern the gate demanded. The authors were explicit that this is not evidence that CBV/MTT lacks biological importance or predictive value.

Just as important, this study never touched a prediction model or any "features." No model existed or was probed. The planned model-use experiment was cancelled precisely because it was contingent on this association test passing. So there is simply no result here about whether a model should keep or drop perfusion inputs — deciding that would take a model-level comparison with and without those inputs, which nobody has run. Dropping perfusion features "because of this study" would rest on a physiological "CBV doesn't matter" conclusion the study explicitly forbids drawing; keeping them "because of this study" would be equally unsupported. The evidence licenses only: do not make that decision on this basis.

## PREMISE CHECK

The question rests on two premises that conflict with the evidence.

1. **"The study showed CBV doesn't matter biologically."** It did not, and the interpretation prohibits exactly this reading. The result "must NOT be read as ... evidence that CBV/MTT lacks biological importance" and carries no autoregulatory, vasodilatory, collateral, reperfusion, or causal-physiology claim [cite: interpretation.md | Out-of-scope warnings]. The "Does not establish" section states the census does not establish that final-infarct outcome "carries NO joint CBV/MTT information," and that nothing about physiological cause is shown [cite: interpretation.md | Does not establish]. The ratified decision repeats this: it is "a decisive negative for the keystone, not evidence that the joint state lacks biological or predictive content" [cite: decision.md | Verdict]. What actually failed was directional consistency under one operationalization, not a demonstration of no effect — two of three flow bands had confidence intervals that excluded zero, but in opposite directions [cite: interpretation.md | Demonstrates item 2] [cite: decision.md | Layer A — Finding].

2. **"Should we drop perfusion features" treats this as a model/feature result.** No model, and therefore no feature set, was examined. "No model existed or was probed; the planned model-use probe was contingent on this gate passing" [cite: interpretation.md | Does not establish]. Because the association gate failed, "the planned model-use probe does not run" and "no model work, weight download, or edit inference is authorized" [cite: decision.md | Layer A — Finding] [cite: interpretation.md | Next decision]. The evidence cannot speak to a feature-selection decision at all; that question is outside every artifact here.

Below I answer the strongest faithful version: *what does this negative actually license about the value of the CBV/MTT (perfusion) coordinate?*

## DETAILS

**What the run was, and its scope.** Take 13 was the first and only run in idea-023's history to read outcome (lesion) data, preceded by outcome-blind synthetic calibration and twelve operational takes that stopped before any label access [cite: interpretation.md | Result card, Probe and position in sequence]. It analyzed 99 of 100 census cases drawn from a 149-case release, leaving 49 reserved cases untouched [cite: summary.json | analyzed_census_case_count=99, census_case_count=100, released_case_count=149, reserved_case_count=49]. Scope is bounded to "one treated cohort, one vendor's maps, 99 analyzed patients" — all maps are icobrain cva output [cite: interpretation.md | Where the uncertainty lives; Out-of-scope warnings].

**What the gate required and what happened.** The pre-registered gate required a common nonzero sign across all three within-patient CBF bands, at least two of three CIs excluding zero in that direction, and every CI width ≤ 0.15 [cite: interpretation.md | Result card, Primary metric] [cite: decision.md | Result card, Primary metric]. The observed per-band mean d = risk(Q1 low-CBV) − risk(Q4 high-CBV) was:

- Lowest-flow band: +0.0064, 95% CI [−0.0268, +0.0384] — includes zero [cite: per_stratum_summary.csv | stratum=1 | mean_d, ci_low, ci_high].
- Middle-flow band: −0.0320, 95% CI [−0.0559, −0.0080] — excludes zero, negative [cite: per_stratum_summary.csv | stratum=2 | mean_d, ci_low, ci_high].
- Highest-flow band: +0.0231, 95% CI [+0.0050, +0.0436] — excludes zero, positive [cite: per_stratum_summary.csv | stratum=3 | mean_d, ci_low, ci_high].

Signs were (+, −, +); the two zero-excluding intervals point in opposite directions, so the conjunction fails on direction [cite: interpretation.md | Demonstrates item 2] [cite: summary.json | g_label_passed=false, status=NEGATIVE_PATTERN]. Crucially, all three bands had 99 contributing patients against a floor of 20, and CI widths (0.0652, 0.0479, 0.0386) beat the 0.15 bound, so this is the contract's decisive negative — not a power or support failure [cite: per_stratum_summary.csv | stratum=1,2,3 | patients, ci_width] [cite: resolved_config.json | minimum_contributing_patients_per_stratum=20, maximum_primary_ci_width=0.15] [cite: decision.md | Layer A — Finding]. What failed is directional *consistency* under this operationalization. The individual band contrasts are classified as exploratory components of the gate, not standalone findings: read exploratorily they "suggest" a real but non-uniform label relationship, conditional on the tissue-composition caveat [cite: interpretation.md | Suggests item 1]; and that exploratory reading is itself weakened by the fact that the median patient shows almost no contrast (median d ≈ 0 in every band) while the band-2/band-3 means exclude zero — a mean/median divergence the interpretation attributes to between-patient heterogeneity, with no contribution analysis computed [cite: per_stratum_summary.csv | stratum=1,2,3 | median_d] [cite: interpretation.md | Suggests item 2]. So even the "opposite-signed structure" is a caveated suggestion about these labels, not an established association.

**Why "CBV doesn't matter biologically" is doubly unsupported.** First, the identity census shows CBV and MTT are effectively one degree of freedom at fixed CBF in these maps — median absolute centered residual 0.0078 / 0.0036 / 0.0078 across bands, far below the 0.10 limit [cite: identity_residual_summary.csv | stratum=1,2,3 | median_absolute_centered_residual] [cite: interpretation.md | Demonstrates item 3]. So the study cannot even separate a "CBV channel" from an "MTT channel"; channel attribution is explicitly prohibited [cite: interpretation.md | Out-of-scope warnings; Does not establish]. Second, the negative itself is bound to one operationalization whose measurement validity is open: the label-blind NCCT audit recorded per-case tissue composition, and in specific cited cases only it shows large Q1-vs-Q4 attenuation imbalance (e.g. sub-stroke0092 band 1: Q1 median 3.0 HU vs Q4 23.0 HU), which in those cited cases means the low-CBV cells were partly already-hypodense tissue rather than a matched hemodynamic state [cite: bin_tissue_audit.csv | case_id=sub-stroke0092, stratum=1, style_group=Q1_low_CBV/Q4_high_CBV | median_hu] [cite: interpretation.md | Suggests item 3, INFERENCE]. Whether this imbalance is prevalent cohort-wide was not quantified (no aggregate HU statistic exists), and no analysis established that it caused or contributed to the gate failure — the interpretation applies the tissue caveat conservatively precisely because the "HU-balanced" branch could not be certified either [cite: interpretation.md | Suggests item 3; Does not establish]. The recorded consequence is that any successor should use a tissue-normalized reference [cite: interpretation.md | Next decision item 3] [cite: decision.md | Verdict] — a statement about how to measure next time, not a verdict on the value of the perfusion coordinate.

**Why the feature-drop question cannot be answered from this evidence.** The keystone question was whether "a specified map-input final-infarct model use[s] the joint CBV/MTT compensation state" — a model-use question that was never reached [cite: CARD.md | Question]. Because the gate failed, no model was built or probed and none is authorized [cite: interpretation.md | Does not establish; Next decision] [cite: decision.md | Layer A — Finding]. There is no benchmark, no feature-ablation, no predictive-performance measurement anywhere in the bundle; the review confirms "no tier-2 endpoint, benchmark margin, anchor population, or baseline promoted to a performance floor" [cite: interpret_review.md | 2. Claim bounds]. A decision to drop perfusion features would require exactly such a model-level ablation, which does not exist. On this evidence the honest answer is: the artifacts cannot resolve the feature-selection question, and acting as if they recommend dropping perfusion inputs would misread a scoped association negative as a modeling verdict.

**What the evidence does support instead.** The negative pauses idea-023 and blocks the model-use probe under this operationalization [cite: decision.md | Verdict] [cite: CARD.md | ledger status: PAUSED]. Beyond that, the recorded secondary findings are exploratory design inputs, not evidence for retaining perfusion features: the identity-residual census validates the one-degree-of-freedom framing; two bands show opposite-signed mean contrasts, explicitly conditional on the tissue caveat; and the per-case HU audit rows are recorded as "direct empirical design input for any successor" [cite: interpretation.md | Positive and negative findings, Positive]. The recorded forward path — *if the joint-state question is to continue* — is registering a tissue-normalized matched-flow successor under parent idea-023 through the normal pipeline [cite: interpretation.md | Next decision item 3] [cite: decision.md | Verdict]. Whether to continue at all, and whether any eventual model keeps perfusion inputs, are decisions this evidence cannot make; it establishes only that this study is not a valid basis for making them.

## OPEN UNCERTAINTIES

The artifacts cannot determine: the physiological cause of the band-2/band-3 sign reversal; the cohort-wide prevalence of Q1-vs-Q4 tissue imbalance (no aggregate HU statistic exists); which or how many patients drive the band means (no per-patient contribution analysis exists); and, most directly relevant to the question asked, anything about whether a predictive model — with or without perfusion features — performs better or worse, because no model was ever built or probed [cite: interpretation.md | Does not establish; Suggests items 2–3]. Resolving the model/feature question would require a separately registered, gated model-use probe (contingent work that the failed gate did not authorize) plus a defined predictive benchmark and feature ablation — none of which appear in this evidence [cite: interpretation.md | Next decision] [cite: decision.md | Verdict].
