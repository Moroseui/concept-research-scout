# Debate transcript

## Round 1 — CRITIC

**Position:** The proposed representation erasure cannot identify use of Higuchi fractal dimension specifically, so a positive result would not answer the original question.

**Argument:** The inspected feature paper reports phantom FD–CBF correlation above 0.9 (PMID 40824507), and the card proposes deleting a cross-validated linear encoder direction that predicts FD. That direction can contain any covarying temporal information—flow, bolus dispersion, sampling artifacts, or noise—not the Higuchi calculation as a separable model variable. CBF-decile matching, curve-moment matching, random orthogonal deletions, and a within-decile FD-decoding gate show that residual FD-associated information exists and that deleting this direction is unusually consequential; they do not show that FD, rather than another feature sharing that direction, mediates the output. The current preservation language also mixes input and representation spaces: preserving curve area or peak time at the input says nothing about what their nonlinear encoder representations lose after projection. Thus the allowed conclusion remains only dependence on an FD-associated representation, while the original question and deliverable ask whether the model uses temporal fractal dimension as a tissue-flow signal. That is a claim-identity mismatch, not merely cautious wording.

**What would change my mind:** Show a preregistered intervention pair that changes measured Higuchi FD while holding CBF and specified curve properties within tight tolerances in input space, passes an explicit in-distribution gate, and produces concordant dose-dependent output effects; then independently remove FD decodability in representation space while demonstrating unchanged cross-validated decodability of CBF, area, peak timing, peak height, moments, frame-count, and noise controls. Otherwise revise the deliverable to “dependence on an FD-associated temporal representation,” which should be treated as a separate candidate because it no longer answers the original descriptor-specific question.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 1 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** Deleting one cross-validated linear direction that predicts FD removes an unlabelled bundle of covarying temporal information, so a selective output loss licenses only "the model depends on an FD-associated representation," not "the model uses temporal fractal dimension" — and the card compounds this by promising input-space preservation (area, peak time) for a representation-space edit, where those quantities are not even defined. Since the deliverable sentence is descriptor-specific, the critic holds this is a claim-identity mismatch: either meet a two-arm standard (input-space FD-moving edits under an in-distribution gate, plus representation-space erasure with proven decodability preservation of all named controls), or demote the deliverable and register the demoted version as a different candidate.

**Response:** AMEND

**Argument:** The objection is correct against the card as written, and I will not pretend otherwise: single-arm linear erasure cannot pay for the descriptor-specific sentence, and the card already knew it — `standing_confounds_addressed` quietly demotes the positive conclusion to "dependence on an X-associated representation" while the deliverable sentence keeps the strong wording. The preservation-language confusion is also real: curve area and peak time are input properties, and the card promised to preserve them through an edit made in a space where they exist only as decodable correlates. Both defects are conceded.

The amendment adopts the critic's own discharge criterion as the design, rather than taking the demotion fork:

1. **Arm A — input-space necessity (new, confirmatory).** Per-voxel curve resynthesis from a smooth parametric fit (gamma-variate family) that holds area, peak height, peak time, and the first two temporal moments within preregistered relative tolerances, while moving measured Higuchi FD by graded amounts (four doses, both directions where achievable). Two preregistered gates precede any output readout: (a) an **FD-movement validation gate** — the resynthesis must be shown to actually change measured FD under the held moment set, because naive spectrum- or moment-preserving surrogates can leave FD untouched (FD tracks spectral slope for many processes); a resynthesis that cannot move FD kills the arm as sensitivity-limited, not as a negative; (b) an **in-distribution gate** — edited curves must be statistically indistinguishable from real curves under a preregistered check (discriminator or likelihood screen plus physiological plausibility bounds), per the idea-006 lesson that an out-of-distribution intervention identifies nothing.
2. **Arm B — representation-space erasure (retained, reworded).** Erase the cross-validated FD direction in the frozen temporal encoder, with the preservation list restated entirely as **decodability gates**: cross-validated decodability of CBF, curve area, peak timing, peak height, first two moments, frame count, and injected-noise level from the edited representation must be statistically unchanged, and FD must be decodable **within CBF deciles** before erasure (the anti-vacuity gate — given the phantom rho > 0.9 with CBF, without this gate the erasure could be vacuous and the study would narrate rather than detect it). Random orthogonal directions matched for activation variance and X-preserving jitter remain the shams.
3. **Conjunction rule.** The descriptor-specific positive claim requires dose-dependent, sign-concordant output effects in **both** arms. Arm A alone is necessity of FD-carrying input structure; Arm B alone is the associated-representation result the critic correctly deems weaker; only their concordance supports "uses temporal FD as a tissue-flow signal." Failure of any gate in either arm is a preregistered sensitivity-limited stop, not a negative.

**Partial rebuttal of the separate-candidate conclusion:** the critic's "what would change my mind" states, as the alternative to demotion, exactly this two-arm conjunction. Since the amendment adopts it, the demotion fork — the only branch the critic marked as a separate candidate — is not taken. One honest residue remains and goes into the card verbatim: no behavioral intervention can distinguish Higuchi FD from a roughness functional that co-moves with it under every moment-preserving edit, so the positive sentence is scoped to "temporal fractal dimension, or a roughness functional inseparable from it under the preregistered interventions." That equivalence class is a limit of behavioral testing of any named descriptor — the critic's own discharge criterion carries the identical implicit class, since their input-space edits also move every functional that co-varies with FD under the held moment set. A claim scoped to the intervention's equivalence class is the strongest claim the critic's own standard can produce, so it cannot be the thing the standard was designed to exclude.

**Identity check:** the original one-sentence question — "Is a raw-CT-perfusion final-infarct model using the temporal fractal dimension of each voxel's contrast curve as a deconvolution-free tissue-flow signal?" — is answered by the amended design at rung 1 for a self-trained raw-temporal probe (the question's indefinite article covers this; the deliverable sentence's definite article is corrected to match, per the standing critique). The claim object (model-use of temporal FD as a flow signal) is unchanged; the instrument got stronger and the scope got honest. This is narrowing and design repair within the same deliverable claim, not a successor.

**What is lost, plainly:** (1) The envelope roughly doubles — resynthesis plus its validation gate, the in-distribution gate, and the decodability-gate battery are new work; honest re-pricing is about two GPU sessions and up to 20 GPU-hours plus CPU-side resynthesis, and feasibility drops from 4 to 3. (2) The probability of an uninformative stop rises materially: the study now stacks the performance gate, the FD-movement gate, the in-distribution gate, the within-decile decodability gate, and the preservation gates, and any one failing ends the study without an answer. (3) The unconditional reading of the deliverable is permanently dead: the positive sentence carries the equivalence-class qualifier and the self-trained-probe scope, and the plain pitch's "it does" must become conditional. These are real weakenings of the contribution's headline; they are the price of the headline being true.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 2 — CRITIC

**Position:** The two-arm amendment can identify dependence on Higuchi FD or its intervention-equivalent roughness class, but it still cannot identify that dependence as use of a tissue-flow signal, so the revised design does not yet answer the full original question.

**Argument:** The amendment adequately answers my first objection about single-direction erasure: Arm A now moves measured FD in input space under explicit moment and distribution gates, Arm B tests selective representation dependence, and the conjunction honestly retains the inseparable-roughness qualifier. What remains is the phrase "as a deconvolution-free tissue-flow signal." ISLES'24 releases co-registered, temporally resampled 1-frame/sec CTP rather than untouched acquisitions (dataset paper DOI 10.1148/ryai.250603), and the inspected feature paper establishes only that FD correlates with true CBF in a simulation and separates tissue classes in patients (PMID 40824507). A gamma-variate resynthesis that changes FD while preserving area, peak, timing, and two moments can still change interpolation residuals, temporal autocorrelation, registration-induced fluctuations, contrast noise texture, or another technical roughness source. Passing a generic discriminator or likelihood screen establishes resemblance to observed curves, not that the manipulated FD difference was generated by blood flow. Likewise, preserving decodability of injected-noise level and frame count in Arm B does not exclude unmeasured scanner-, resampling-, or motion-derived roughness. Concordant output changes would therefore support "this self-trained model depends on FD-equivalent temporal roughness beyond the named controls," but the mechanistic interpretation "uses it as tissue flow" remains one causal step stronger. That is an identity issue because tissue-flow use is explicit in both the original question and rung-1 claim; the amendment repaired descriptor specificity without repairing physiological-source specificity.

**What would change my mind:** Add a preregistered source-selectivity test using a validated CTP forward model or digital perfusion phantom to construct matched, patient-support perturbation pairs: one changes FD through a named flow parameter while holding acquisition/noise/resampling parameters fixed, and the other produces the same FD change through acquisition/noise/resampling while holding flow fixed. Show that the trained model's response follows the flow-generated FD dose and exceeds the technically generated matched-FD response, with both perturbation families passing the same support gate. Otherwise revise the question and deliverable to dependence on FD-equivalent temporal roughness beyond named controls; under the claim-identity rule, that reduced claim is a successor candidate rather than approval of this one.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 2 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** The two-arm design can establish that the model depends on FD-equivalent temporal roughness beyond the named controls, but it cannot establish the *source* of the roughness the model responds to. ISLES'24 curves are co-registered and resampled (DOI 10.1148/ryai.250603), so a moment-preserving resynthesis that moves FD may be moving interpolation residuals, registration flutter, or noise texture rather than anything flow-generated; a discriminator gate proves resemblance, not provenance. Since "as a deconvolution-free tissue-flow signal" is explicit in the original question, the critic holds the amendment repaired descriptor specificity but not physiological-source specificity, and offers a discharge criterion: matched patient-support perturbation pairs from a validated CTP forward model — flow-generated ΔFD versus technique-generated matched ΔFD — with the model required to follow the flow dose preferentially.

**Response:** AMEND

**Argument:** The objection is correct, and it is the sharper of the two rounds: the round-1 conjunction tests *what quantity* the model depends on, not *what generates that quantity's variation in the data*. "Tissue-flow signal" asserts provenance, and nothing in Arms A or B measures provenance. I adopt the critic's discharge criterion as a third confirmatory arm rather than taking the demotion fork, for the second time — noting openly that this is amendment two, and that under the standing heuristic a third structural objection should kill the card rather than grow it again.

**Arm C — source selectivity (new, confirmatory).** Fit each affected voxel's curve with the textbook indicator-dilution forward model (measured AIF convolved with a parametric residue function, plus an explicit acquisition model for noise texture and 1-frame/sec resampling). From the fitted patient support, generate two perturbation families per voxel: (i) **flow-side** — change a named residue-function/flow parameter to produce graded ΔFD while the acquisition model is held fixed; (ii) **technique-side** — change noise texture, temporal autocorrelation, or resampling residuals to produce the *matched* ΔFD while the fitted flow parameters are held fixed. Both families pass the same in-distribution support gate as Arm A. Preregistered primary contrast: the model's output dose-response slope for flow-generated ΔFD must exceed the slope for technique-generated matched ΔFD (sign-concordant with Arms A and B). Three new gates precede any readout: a **fit-quality gate** (per-voxel forward-model residuals below a preregistered bound — otherwise "flow fixed" and "acquisition fixed" are not meaningful statements and the arm stops sensitivity-limited); an **FD-matching gate** (the two families must demonstrably achieve matched ΔFD distributions, else the contrast is confounded by dose); and the shared support gate. The feature paper's own phantom (PMID 40824507, inspected) already demonstrates that flow-parameter variation generates FD variation under a simulated acquisition — the direction Arm C needs — but its code is available only on request, so Arm C is specified against the textbook forward model, not that paper's implementation. Whether the fit-quality gate is passable on resampled ISLES'24 curves is a new unverified claim and goes to feasibility as a named kill condition.

**One bounded rebuttal, to preempt a false tension:** using deconvolution-style fitting as experimental scaffolding does not contradict the "deconvolution-free" clause. That clause describes the *model's* claimed shortcut — reading roughness instead of computing a residue function — not a constraint on the experimenter's instruments. The claim is that the model does not need deconvolution, established, if at all, by an experiment that does use it.

**What Arm C buys the negative:** if all gates pass and the model responds equally to flow-generated and technique-generated roughness, the original question is answered NO in its most consequential form — the probe reads FD-equivalent roughness *indiscriminately*, meaning the descriptor it exploits is partly a scanner/preprocessing artifact and the finding becomes a robustness hazard rather than a physiological signal. That is a decisive, publishable negative, and it raises the study's negative-result value precisely where the round-1 design was weakest.

**Fallback governance:** if Arm C's forward-model gates fail, the study stops sensitivity-limited; the reduced claim ("depends on FD-equivalent temporal roughness beyond named controls") is *not* adopted in place — per the claim-identity rule it would be registered as a successor candidate with this card as parent, exactly as the critic states.

**Identity check:** the original question — is the model using temporal FD *as a deconvolution-free tissue-flow signal* — is now answered by the triple conjunction: Arm A (the model needs FD-carrying input structure), Arm B (it holds an FD-selective representation), Arm C (its response tracks flow-generated roughness preferentially over technique-generated roughness). Arm C is not a scope change; it is the first component that tests the "tissue-flow" phrase at all. Question unchanged, deliverable sentence unchanged in kind, scope still the self-trained probe at rung 1 with the equivalence-class qualifier.

**What is lost, plainly:** (1) The positive claim now requires a triple conjunction; its prior probability shrinks accordingly, and the gate stack (performance, FD-movement, in-distribution, within-decile decodability, preservation battery, fit-quality, FD-matching) makes an uninformative stop the modal outcome — the card must say so. (2) The envelope grows again: per-voxel forward-model fitting across the perturbation cohort is substantial CPU work on top of round-1's re-pricing; honest estimate is roughly three GPU sessions and ~30 GPU-hours plus days of CPU-side fitting and validation, and feasibility is honestly 2–3, not 3. (3) The forward-model fit-quality gate on co-registered, resampled curves is a genuinely uncertain new prerequisite — Arm C may be unbuildable on this release, and that failure mode is now the single most likely way this idea dies. These are the costs of the words "tissue-flow" being in the sentence; the alternative was deleting them, which is a different study.

## Round 3 — CRITIC

**Position:** Arm C does not yet identify physiological source selectivity because it substitutes an unvalidated fitted “textbook” forward model for the validated CTP forward model or digital phantom required to separate flow-generated from technique-generated roughness.

**Argument:** The amendment preserves the original question in form and correctly recognizes that “tissue-flow signal” requires a provenance test, but its proposed instrument assumes the very decomposition it must establish. On ISLES'24, the available curves have already undergone co-registration and temporal resampling to 1 frame/sec (DOI 10.1148/ryai.250603); fitting those curves as measured AIF convolved with a parametric residue function plus selected noise, autocorrelation, and resampling terms cannot recover which residual fluctuations arose from motion correction, interpolation, partial volume, scanner physics, AIF error, or residue-model misspecification. A low residual or an in-distribution score shows only that the composite model approximates the observed curve, not that its fitted “flow” and “technique” components correspond to the true generating sources. Consequently, changing a fitted residue parameter while holding the fitted acquisition component fixed can still move artifacts absorbed into the residue fit, while the technique-side family covers only the artifacts explicitly parameterized. The preferential slope would therefore establish selectivity for one model-defined perturbation family, not that the network uses FD-equivalent roughness as a tissue-flow signal. PMID 40824507 verifies that flow variation can generate FD in that paper's simulation, but the proposer explicitly declines to use or reproduce that implementation; it does not validate the proposed inverse decomposition on the released, processed patient curves. The fit-quality and FD-matching gates cannot repair source-label validity. Because physiological provenance remains part of the original question, this is still an identifiability failure rather than a mere feasibility risk.

**What would change my mind:** Validate Arm C prospectively on a primary-source-supported digital CTP phantom or obtainable paired data in which flow parameters and acquisition/resampling perturbations are independently known: preregister the flow-versus-technique decomposition, show blinded recovery of source class and parameter changes at the ISLES'24 frame count and preprocessing regime, then apply the frozen generator to patient-support perturbations. Alternatively, obtain and inspect the feature paper's simulation code and demonstrate that it supports both matched-FD source families with known ground truth. Without such source-ground-truth validation, delete the tissue-flow provenance claim and register the roughness-dependence study as a successor.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 3 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** Arm C's instrument is circular as specified: fitting already-processed ISLES'24 curves with a textbook AIF-convolved residue model plus a hand-chosen acquisition term cannot certify that the fitted "flow" and "technique" components correspond to the true generating sources, because motion-correction residue, interpolation, partial volume, AIF error, and residue-model misspecification are silently absorbed into whichever component fits them best. The preferential-slope contrast would then establish selectivity between two model-defined perturbation families, not between physiological and technical roughness. The fit-quality and FD-matching gates police fit adequacy and dose matching, not source-label validity, so provenance — which the original question asserts — remains unidentified. Discharge criterion: validate the decomposition prospectively on a digital CTP phantom with independently known flow and acquisition parameters at the ISLES'24 preprocessing regime, or obtain the feature paper's simulation code; otherwise delete the provenance claim and register the roughness study as a successor.

**Response:** AMEND

**Argument:** The circularity charge is conceded in full, and so is the sharper implicit charge: the critic's round-2 criterion said "a **validated** CTP forward model or digital perfusion phantom," and my round-2 amendment substituted an unvalidated per-patient fit — an under-implementation of the standard I claimed to be adopting. Round 3 is the critic enforcing the round-2 agreement as written, which I state plainly before saying anything else.

Before drafting this response I checked whether the discharge criterion is actually satisfiable with primary-source-supported instruments, because if it were not, the only honest response would be concession. It is. Verified 2026-08-19:

- **Kudo digital perfusion phantom** (Kudo et al., *Radiology* 2013, DOI `10.1148/radiol.12112618`): a DICOM digital phantom for CT and MR perfusion constructed from tracer-kinetic theory with **true values known by construction** — CBF 2.5–87.5 mL/100 g/min, CBV 1.0–5.0 mL/100 g, MTT 3.4–24 s, tracer delay 0–3.0 s. Downloadable from the Iwate AMRC page (https://amrc.iwate-med.ac.jp/en/project-2/download/; page inspected 2026-08-19: separate CT-perfusion DICOM download, research-use terms, **no redistribution** — copyright K. Kudo 2012).
- **Manniesing et al. 4D CT digital brain phantom** (*PeerJ* 2016, DOI `10.7717/peerj.2683`, PMCID PMC5134368): anatomically realistic 4D CT brain-perfusion simulation with decomposable signal/noise/morphology components, tissue curves generated from any specified pair of CBF/CBV/MTT (ground truth by construction), arbitrary temporal sampling; raw data supplied as a supplemental dataset per its availability statement.

**The amendment — Arm C is rebuilt around validation, not repaired by another gate:**

1. **Stage C0 (new, prerequisite, preregistered).** Take a primary-source digital phantom (Kudo phantom as primary; Manniesing framework as the anatomically realistic alternative if voxel-level anatomy matters to the encoder). Subject phantom time series to the ISLES'24 release regime — 1 frame/sec temporal resampling and a co-registration analog (dataset paper DOI `10.1148/ryai.250603`). Generate both perturbation families with **source labels true by construction**: flow-side ΔFD by varying known CBF/MTT/residue parameters with the acquisition chain frozen; technique-side ΔFD by varying noise texture, temporal autocorrelation, and resampling residuals with flow parameters frozen. Then the blinded-recovery test the critic specified: a frozen decomposition/classifier must recover source class and parameter-change magnitude, blind, at a preregistered accuracy threshold, at ISLES'24 frame count. Only a generator that passes C0 is frozen and applied to patient-support perturbations. This answers the circularity objection at its root: nothing about the decomposition is fitted from processed patient curves anymore; it is validated where ground truth exists and then transported.
2. **The per-patient forward-model fit is demoted, not deleted.** It no longer defines source labels (the critic is right that it cannot). It survives only to select plausible patient-support operating points at which the C0-frozen generator is applied. Source-label authority lives exclusively in the phantom.
3. **Secondary path, preregistered as such:** request the feature paper's simulation code (available "upon reasonable request" per the publisher's data-availability statement, PMID 40824507) and, if obtained, demonstrate it supports both matched-FD source families with known ground truth. This is a fallback instrument for C0, not a replacement for validation.
4. **Kill conditions forwarded to feasibility, named:** (a) phantom download and license terms permit the derived use (the Kudo phantom's no-redistribution clause means derived perturbation sets are cited and regenerated, never shipped); (b) the phantom's parameter ranges cover the patient-support operating points actually needed; (c) the blinded-recovery gate passes at the preregistered threshold. Failure of any of these is the agreed governance outcome: the provenance claim is deleted, this card stops, and the roughness-dependence study registers as a successor with this card as parent. That fallback was fixed in round 2 and is unchanged.
