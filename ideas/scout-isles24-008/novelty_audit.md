# Novelty audit — scouting cycle isles24-008

## C1 — The ghost core and the water clock: what the NCCT-only winner knows that the perfusion thresholds don't (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Broocks et al., “Low baseline ischemic water uptake is directly related to overestimation of CT perfusion-derived ischemic core volume,” *European Radiology* 2022, PMCID `PMC9708677`: tested whether low admission NWU predicts CTP-core overestimation. (ii) “Feasibility of net water uptake for CTP predicting true infarct core and long-term outcome in acute ischemic stroke,” *Journal of Radiation Research and Applied Sciences* 2026, DOI `10.1016/j.jrras.2026.102324`: stratified CBF/CBV thresholds by NWU and externally tested prediction of true core and outcome. (iii) “Fully automated quantification of net water uptake in acute ischemic stroke using only non-contrast CT imaging,” *European Radiology Experimental* 2025, PMCID `PMC13212784`: automated NCCT-only NWU and compared its lesion estimates with CTP/DWI references.
2. **Delta.** The candidate audits whether an NCCT-only learned model's *voxelwise disagreement* with a CTP threshold is conditionally organized by NWU; the neighbors establish NWU measurement and NWU-conditioned threshold error, but do not behaviorally interrogate a trained final-infarct model.
3. **Why not done.** `NEW_CAPABILITY` — ISLES'24 supplies co-registered NCCT, perfusion maps, and follow-up infarct labels, while the 2025 winning recipe supplies the unusual NCCT-only model whose disagreement can now be interrogated.
4. **Verdict.** `INCREMENTAL`. The model-behavior readout is new in the searched literature, but the scientific relationship “NWU explains CTP core overestimation” is already directly tested, including a 2026 multicenter threshold-stratification study.

## C2 — The unpredictable infarct: how much of the benchmark happened after the scan (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Kang et al., “Incidence of new diffusion-weighted imaging lesions outside the area of initial hypoperfusion within 1 week after acute ischemic stroke,” *Stroke* 2012, PMID `22851548`: manually identified new DWI lesions outside initial MTT deficits in 164 patients and found them in 23%. (ii) Kaesmacher et al., “Infarct in new territory after endovascular stroke treatment: A diffusion-weighted imaging study,” *Scientific Reports* 2020, DOI `10.1038/s41598-020-64437-0` (PMCID `PMC7239894`): defined and measured infarcts in initially unaffected territories after EVT. (iii) Ganesh et al., “Infarcts in a New Territory: Insights From the ESCAPE-NA1 Trial,” *Stroke* 2023, PMID `37082967`: core-lab identified follow-up infarcts outside baseline target occlusions in a thrombectomy trial.
2. **Delta.** The candidate converts this known outside-lesion phenomenon into an ISLES'24 benchmark audit, quantifying unsupported ground-truth volume and the induced ceiling/floor in the challenge metrics; that is mainly an evaluation-context delta, not a new biological observation.
3. **Why not done.** `NEW_CAPABILITY` — the public ISLES'24 release places acute perfusion maps and delayed final-infarct masks on one grid, permitting a reproducible benchmark-wide support census without institutional data access.
4. **Verdict.** `INCREMENTAL`. The central phenomenon and nearly the same geometry comparison were published in 2012; the worthwhile remaining contribution is the dataset-specific metric audit.

## C3 — Written off by depth: the internal borderzone in the model's triage (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Phan et al., “Dynamics of regional distribution of ischemic lesions in middle cerebral artery trunk occlusion relates to collateral circulation,” *Journal of Cerebral Blood Flow & Metabolism* 2011, DOI `10.1038/jcbfm.2010.177` (PMCID `PMC3049469`): mapped central-to-peripheral tissue fate and related the regional pattern to collateral anatomy. (ii) Ho et al., “Use of Deep Learning to Predict Final Ischemic Stroke Lesions From Initial Magnetic Resonance Imaging,” *JAMA Network Open* 2020, DOI `10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.0772`: trained a U-Net for final-lesion prediction and explicitly noted tissue class and collaterals as sources of heterogeneous susceptibility. (iii) Reidler et al., “Automated Prediction of Ischemic Brain Tissue Fate from Multiphase Computed Tomographic Angiography in Patients with Acute Ischemic Stroke Using Machine Learning,” *Frontiers in Neurology* 2021, DOI `10.3389/fneur.2021.644976` (PMCID `PMC8189856`): predicted perfusion abnormality and follow-up infarction from time-resolved pial vascular images.
2. **Delta.** None of these works reports a within-patient, perfusion-and-tissue-matched audit of a final-infarct model against continuous distance from the pial surface; the delta is a behavioral decomposition rather than a new predictor or model.
3. **Why not done.** `BLIND_SPOT` — final-infarct studies optimize accuracy and report aggregate or territory-level results, while the collateral literature describes regional vulnerability; neither has treated cortical depth as a model-behavior coordinate under matched perfusion.
4. **Verdict.** `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH`. Three close biological/modeling neighbors were distinguished, but terminology varies (“central/peripheral,” “deep,” “borderzone,” “regional distribution”), preventing a high-confidence absence claim.

## C4 — The blood arrives backwards: filling direction as the model's collateral gauge (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Menon et al., “Multiphase CT angiography: a new tool for the imaging triage of patients with acute ischemic stroke,” *Radiology* 2015, DOI `10.1148/radiol.15142256`: introduced multiphase CTA assessment of delayed/retrograde pial collateral filling. (ii) Smit et al., “Assessment of Collateral Status by Dynamic CT Angiography in Acute MCA Stroke: Timing of Acquisition and Relationship with Final Infarct Volume,” *AJNR* 2015, PMCID `PMC7960354`: showed that extent and velocity of dynamic collateral filling improve prediction of final infarct volume over a single CTA phase. (iii) Su et al., “Time dependency of automated collateral scores in computed tomography angiography and computed tomography perfusion images in patients with intracranial arterial occlusion,” *Neuroradiology* 2023, DOI `10.1007/s00234-022-03050-4` (PMCID `PMC9859867`): applied automated collateral scoring across CTP time points and demonstrated acquisition-time dependence.
2. **Delta.** The candidate turns Tmax into a spatial arrival-direction vector field and tests whether a final-infarct model's spatial predictions follow that field; the neighbors score collateral extent/timing but do not compute gradient–depth alignment or audit model use of it.
3. **Why not done.** `BLIND_SPOT` — collateral work treats time as phase/velocity and perfusion work treats Tmax as a scalar severity map; the vector-field interpretation lies between those conventions.
4. **Verdict.** `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH`. No duplicate was located, but the proposed FDI terminology is new and searches may miss mathematically equivalent arrival-field analyses.

## C5 — The rough edge grows: boundary geometry as the model's stability gauge (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Phan et al., “Dynamics of regional distribution of ischemic lesions in middle cerebral artery trunk occlusion relates to collateral circulation,” *Journal of Cerebral Blood Flow & Metabolism* 2011, DOI `10.1038/jcbfm.2010.177`: related spatial infarct evolution to collateral supply, but did not measure boundary roughness. (ii) Ho et al., “Use of Deep Learning to Predict Final Ischemic Stroke Lesions From Initial Magnetic Resonance Imaging,” *JAMA Network Open* 2020, DOI `10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.0772`: predicted final lesions from baseline imaging without a contour-roughness mechanism audit. (iii) Hu et al., “Association of Perfusion Lesion Variables With Functional Outcome in Patients With Mild Stroke and Large Vessel Occlusion Managed Medically,” *Journal of the American Heart Association* 2023, DOI `10.1161/JAHA.122.027189` (PMCID `PMC9946183`): assessed perfusion-lesion measurements against outcome, centered on lesion extent rather than multiscale contour morphology.
2. **Delta.** The candidate uniquely proposes multiscale Tmax-contour roughness plus boundary-local growth as a model-behavior test, but the physiological interpretation as an unstable collateral-pressure front is not established by any neighbor.
3. **Why not done.** `BLIND_SPOT` — stroke radiomics largely treats shape features as anonymous predictors, while interface-roughening theory has not been connected to perfusion-lesion contours.
4. **Verdict.** `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH`. Searches found no close roughness study, but that scarcity weakens rather than strengthens confidence: the nearest works are conceptually distant, and shape/radiomics terminology is heterogeneous.

## C6 — The forecaster's hedge: the model's volume is a bet, not a belief (wide)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Bertels et al., “Optimization with soft Dice can lead to a volumetric bias,” MICCAI BrainLes 2019, arXiv `1911.02278`: theoretically and empirically showed uncertainty-dependent volume bias from soft-Dice optimization on four medical tasks. (ii) Bertels et al., “Theoretical analysis and experimental validation of volume bias of soft Dice optimized segmentation maps in the context of inherent uncertainty,” *Medical Image Analysis* 2021, DOI `10.1016/j.media.2020.101833` (arXiv `2211.04161`): showed CE probability sums yield unbiased volumes while soft Dice can bias volume and recommended recalibration. (iii) Dai and Li, “RankSEG: A Consistent Ranking-based Framework for Segmentation,” *JMLR* 2023, arXiv `2206.13086`: derived Bayes/plug-in ranking rules for expected Dice/IoU rather than fixed 0.5 thresholding.
2. **Delta.** The candidate combines these established results into a case-level revealed-policy audit on ISLES'24, asking whether committed volume tracks the expected-Dice-optimal rank rule rather than probability mass; the theoretical mechanism and CE-versus-Dice contrast are not new.
3. **Why not done.** `BLIND_SPOT` — calibration and Dice-bias papers frame the issue as estimation error or a method to correct, not as an auditable forecasting policy linked to a stroke benchmark's AVD endpoint.
4. **Verdict.** `INCREMENTAL`. The proposed empirical packaging is useful, but its causal ablation, signed prediction, and optimal-decision comparator are direct applications of closely matching prior theory.

## C7 — Caught mid-swallow: does the model read acquisition luck as collateral biology? (wide)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Smit et al., “Assessment of Collateral Status by Dynamic CT Angiography in Acute MCA Stroke: Timing of Acquisition and Relationship with Final Infarct Volume,” *AJNR* 2015, PMCID `PMC7960354`: compared dynamic and single-phase collateral assessment and related filling timing to final infarct. (ii) Su et al., “Time dependency of automated collateral scores in computed tomography angiography and computed tomography perfusion images in patients with intracranial arterial occlusion,” *Neuroradiology* 2023, DOI `10.1007/s00234-022-03050-4`: demonstrated that an automated collateral score changes across CTP acquisition time. (iii) Kokk et al., “Factors influencing the reliability of a CT angiography-based deep learning method for infarct volume estimation,” *European Radiology Experimental* 2024, DOI `10.1186/s41747-024-00419-7` (PMCID `PMC10860582`): examined a CTA CNN and measured CTA timing against CTP time-attenuation curves, noting nearly all scans were mid-arterial and timing could contribute to limited reliability.
2. **Delta.** The candidate performs a within-patient intervention on a final-infarct model by substituting real CTP frames into a constant pseudo-CTA rendering while holding the full CTP input fixed; the neighbors observe timing dependence but do not causally sweep the model's snapshot channel.
3. **Why not done.** `NEW_CAPABILITY` — ISLES'24 publicly co-releases registered single-phase CTA, full 4D CTP, arterial masks, and delayed infarct labels for the same 149 cases, enabling acquired-frame substitution outside a hospital archive.
4. **Verdict.** `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE`. Multiple lines of directly adjacent clinical, automated-scoring, and CTA-CNN work establish the timing problem, but the searched primary literature did not perform the proposed within-patient model intervention.

## C8 — The model speaks one vendor's dialect: swap the deconvolution, keep the patient (wide)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Kudo et al., “Differences in CT Perfusion Maps Generated by Different Commercial Software: Quantitative Analysis by Using Identical Source Data of Acute Stroke Patients,” *Radiology* 2010, DOI `10.1148/radiol.254082000`: processed identical source data with five packages plus sSVD/bSVD and found large algorithm-class differences in CBF/MTT abnormalities. (ii) Fahmi et al., “Differences in CT Perfusion Summary Maps for Patients with Acute Ischemic Stroke Generated by 2 Software Packages,” *AJNR* 2012, DOI `10.3174/ajnr.A3110` (PMCID `PMC7965595`): found large disagreement in core and penumbra areas from delay-sensitive versus delay-insensitive software on identical data. (iii) Wu et al., “Tracer arrival timing-insensitive technique for estimating flow in MR perfusion-weighted imaging using singular value decomposition with a block-circulant deconvolution matrix,” *Magnetic Resonance in Medicine* 2003, DOI `10.1002/mrm.10522`: introduced block-circulant SVD and showed its reduced tracer-delay sensitivity relative to standard SVD.
2. **Delta.** The candidate feeds paired maps from published deconvolution classes into the *same trained final-infarct model* and compares prediction shifts against within-dialect regularization jitter; prior work stops at map/threshold disagreement.
3. **Why not done.** `NEW_CAPABILITY` — ISLES'24 releases registered raw 4D CTP, vendor-derived maps, arterial masks, and outcome masks together, making a public cohort-scale round-robin through one model possible.
4. **Verdict.** `NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE`. The map-level premise is old and well replicated, but a multi-source search found no primary study measuring paired downstream segmentation-model behavior under the deconvolution swap.
