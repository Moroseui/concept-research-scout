Wrote `ideas/scout-007/fiction_story.md` (~730 words), titled "The Film Remembers."

The story follows Mara Okonkwo, who builds a dataset-bias classifier that identifies which benchmark a retinal vessel mask came from — and works far too well. The saliency maps lead her to a 210-micron periodic corrugation traced only in STARE's arterioles, which she reads as Mönckeberg-style medial calcification: the retina acting as a readout of aortic calcification, recorded by STARE's 1970s film-based TopCon camera and erased by the modern digital cameras' onboard sharpening in DRIVE and CHASE.

Both seed concepts are load-bearing — the scanner vendor signature is the initial hypothesis *and* the doubt she must defeat, and aortic calcification is the discovery. The twist card is honored by the film camera seeing what the digital ones smooth away. The required verification scene is concrete: she splits arterioles from venules on the same film (an instrument artifact would corrugate both; only arterioles show the peak, 11.4× power ratio), then checks against STARE's diagnosis codes (14/14 positives have vascular disease, 6/6 flats are normal, AUC 0.96), and the story ends two paragraphs later.
