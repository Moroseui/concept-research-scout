# Debate transcript

## Round 1 — CRITIC

**Position:** The proposed repair no longer answers the original question: BAC-pixel sensitivity is not evidence that Mirai uses BAC as a vascular-age signal independently of chronological age and breast density.

**Argument:** The card's original claim is mechanistic and conditional—Mirai reads medial arterial calcium *as systemic vascular age*, beyond age and density—whereas the revised primary experiment only compares frozen Mirai scores after BAC inpainting versus a matched sham. A positive paired score change would establish, at most, that pixels selected by a BAC segmenter affect the score. It cannot distinguish the original vascular-clock explanation from the closest prior-supported alternative: Mirai treats coarse arterial calcium as generic calcification evidence or lesion-like microcalcification. That alternative is especially live because the same-model EMBED study, *Using Explainable AI to Characterize Features in the Mirai Model* (Radiology: AI, DOI 10.1148/ryai.240417), already reports calcification-feature reliance. Age matching does not repair this identity break: it controls chronological age but supplies no independent measurement of vascular age, and an equal-area/equal-intensity non-arterial edit tests edit specificity rather than whether the BAC effect encodes systemic vascular ageing. The defensible replacement sentence—"Mirai's risk output responds specifically to breast arterial calcification"—is useful, but it is a different rung-1 question and should be spawned as a separate candidate unless the title, question, and claimed endpoint are formally narrowed.

**What would change my mind:** Show a design with an independently computed vascular-age endpoint (for example, a validated cardiovascular-risk or arterial-age measure unavailable to Mirai), demonstrate that the BAC-dependent component of Mirai's score tracks that endpoint within narrow chronological-age and density strata, and include an intervention that isolates arterial BAC from matched parenchymal calcification; otherwise re-register the project as the narrower BAC-sensitivity candidate.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 1 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** The inpainting-vs-sham design can show that BAC pixels move Mirai's score, but it cannot show that Mirai reads them *as systemic vascular age* rather than as generic or lesion-like calcification — especially given ryai.240417 already established calcification-feature reliance. So either the candidate acquires an independent vascular-age endpoint, or it must formally narrow its title, question, and claimed endpoint to the BAC-sensitivity claim; and the critic leans toward the narrowed version being a separate candidate.

**Response:** AMEND

**Argument:** The critic is right that no design available here identifies vascular-age encoding, and I am not going to pretend one is. The independent endpoint the critic asks for — a cardiovascular-risk or arterial-age measure unavailable to Mirai, linked to the same mammograms — does not exist in any cohort we can currently confirm: EMBED has cancer outcomes and demographics, not cardiovascular linkage, and no public four-view FFDM set carries CAC scores or CVD outcomes. Rung 3 as originally worded is therefore out of reach on obtainable data, not merely unproven. I take the critic's second branch: formal narrowing, which the critic's own position statement allows ("unless the title, question, and claimed endpoint are formally narrowed").

The card changes as follows:

- **Title:** "A breast-cancer risk model may be reading the arteries" — the words "as a vascular clock" are deleted.
- **Question:** "Does frozen Mirai's five-year risk score respond specifically to breast arterial calcification — beyond matched benign parenchymal calcification, chronological age, and breast density — on the same four-view mammograms?"
- **Deliverable sentence:** "Mirai's breast-cancer risk output responds specifically to breast arterial calcification — the linear tram-track calcium in mammary arteries — and not merely to calcification in general."
- **Rung target:** 2, down from 3. "Vascular-age signal" moves to an explicitly labelled rung-3 extension with its named missing instrument: a cardiovascular endpoint (CAC, MESA-style arterial-age, or CVD outcome linkage) joined to a Mirai-compatible mammography cohort. Until such a linkage is confirmed to exist, that extension is aspirational and unclaimed.
- **Primary readout, sharpened to meet the critic's third demand:** paired BAC inpainting against *two* shams — (a) deletion of matched benign parenchymal calcification of equal conspicuity within the same age/density stratum, and (b) deletion of equal-area non-calcific linear structure. Sham (a) is precisely the intervention the critic asked for to isolate arterial from parenchymal calcification. One point of partial disagreement: the critic characterizes the sham arm as testing only "edit specificity." With sham (a) in place it does more — if BAC deletion moves the score significantly more than deletion of equally conspicuous benign parenchymal calcium, the "generic calcification evidence" alternative from ryai.240417 is directly weakened, which is exactly the arterial-vs-lesion delta the critique identified as the open novelty. That is a real discrimination, not just edit hygiene. What it still cannot do is elevate the finding to vascular age, and the amended endpoint no longer says it does.

**Identity check.** Does the amended design still answer the original one-sentence question? Not the full sentence: the clause "as a vascular-age signal" is abandoned, not deferred-and-answered. But the charter's deliverable requirement is "the model is using X, where X is a named anatomical, physiological, or physical thing" — and X = breast arterial calcification satisfies that in full. The vascular-clock wording was a mechanistic interpretation layered on top of the charter-level claim, and the card's own rung ladder already gated everything through BAC-specific erasure before any vascular-age assertion. This is the idea-007 situation (rung 3 → rung 1/2 demotion in place, same candidate), not the idea-003 situation (feasibility achieved by abandoning the motivating intervention). The motivating observation — a breast-cancer score and a cardiovascular imaging marker computed from the same pixels, never connected in print — survives intact.

**Why not a fork:** the narrowed candidate shares the original's keystone (Mirai-tensor/BAC-mask registration on a compatible cohort), assets, cohort, confounds, and medical motivation; the critique already answered "SHOULD IT BECOME A SEPARATE CANDIDATE? NO." A fork would duplicate a ledger entry without changing a single experiment.

**What is lost, plainly:** the headline. "A breast model found a vascular clock" was a rung-3 mechanism claim and the most interesting sentence in the card; it is gone unless a cardiovascular linkage materializes. Interest drops from 5 to 4. What remains is still consequential and physician-legible: if a widely evaluated risk model's output is partly driven by benign arterial calcium, then women with vascular disease receive inflated breast-cancer risk estimates — a calibration and equity finding that stands on its own. Amendment count: one.

**Status:** OPEN

## Round 2 — CRITIC

**Position:** The amendment explicitly abandons the original vascular-age question, so the narrower BAC-sensitivity study must be registered as a separate candidate rather than treated as a revision of Idea 015.

**Argument:** The proposer concedes the decisive identity fact: the amended design does not answer whether Mirai uses BAC *as a vascular-age signal independently of chronological age and density*; it asks only whether BAC inpainting changes Mirai more than matched parenchymal-calcification and linear-structure edits. Shared data, code, masks, and motivation do not preserve a scientific question when the distinguishing causal interpretation has been removed. The two-sham intervention is a credible way to distinguish arterial-location/morphology sensitivity from generic calcification sensitivity, and DOI 10.1148/ryai.240417 makes that delta worthwhile, but even a positive result cannot distinguish vascular ageing from lesion-mimicry or another BAC-correlated image feature. The charter permits rung demotion when a higher rung remains the same eventual claim; here the proposer says the defining clause is "abandoned" and requires a new, presently unavailable cardiovascular endpoint. Under the debate rule, that is replacement rather than repair. Idea 015 should therefore be paused or rejected on its stated question, while the defensible rung-1/2 BAC-response experiment is spawned under a new title and claim. The additional assertion that BAC-driven score changes would mean women with vascular disease receive *inflated* risk estimates also exceeds the label-free intervention: without cancer outcomes and calibration analysis, the study establishes score sensitivity, not miscalibration.

**What would change my mind:** Either provide a Mirai-compatible cohort with an independently measured cardiovascular-age endpoint and a design that links the BAC-dependent score component to that endpoint beyond chronological age and density, thereby restoring the original question, or create a separate candidate whose preregistered endpoint is only BAC-specific score response and whose conclusions avoid claims of vascular-age encoding or inflated cancer risk.

**Status:** IRREDUCIBLE DISAGREEMENT

## Round 2 — PROPOSER

**Responding to:** Because round 1 conceded that the amended design does not answer the vascular-age clause, the debate rule makes the amendment a replacement rather than a repair: Idea 015 should be paused or rejected on its stated question and the BAC-sensitivity experiment re-registered under a new title. Separately, the round-1 claim that BAC-driven score changes would mean women with vascular disease receive *inflated* risk estimates exceeds what a label-free intervention can establish.

**Response:** REBUT — on registration; CONCEDE on the miscalibration wording.

**Argument:** The concession first, because it is simply correct: "inflated risk estimates" asserts miscalibration, and a label-free intervention establishes score sensitivity only. The phrase is struck from the card's motivation. The calibration/equity implication becomes a labelled downstream question requiring outcome-linked calibration analysis, unclaimed — exactly parallel to how the vascular-age extension is handled. With that struck, the amended card's preregistered endpoint is BAC-specific score response and its conclusions avoid both vascular-age encoding and inflated-risk claims — which is, verbatim, the object the critic's second "what would change my mind" branch describes. The residue of this debate is therefore not what study to run or what it may conclude; it is which ledger row the study lives in.

On that residue, three arguments the critic has not answered:

1. **The charter defines this configuration as one candidate, not two.** "A candidate that can reach only rung 2 is allowed, but must say so and must name what would be needed to reach rung 3." That is the amended card's exact structure: rung target 2, vascular-age retained as the labelled rung-3 target, missing instrument named (a cardiovascular endpoint joined to a Mirai-compatible cohort). If honest rung demotion within a candidate constituted replacement, this charter clause could never be exercised — every card that discovered its rung-3 clause was unreachable would fork rather than "say so." My round-1 word "abandoned" described the *claim at the current rung*, and the critic is entitled to hold me to sloppy wording; but the card itself defers the clause with a named instrument, which is the charter's mandated form, not deletion from the program. Candidate identity under the charter is the model and the named X. Both are unchanged: the original question's X is breast arterial calcification, and so is the amended question's.

2. **The record already treats this situation as revision-in-place.** Idea 007's card claimed a nonexistent model output and was demoted rung 3 → rung 1 — "sensitivity to respiratory state, not identification of total lung volume as the cue" — and the decision ledger recorded ADVANCE TO REVISION for the same idea, not a fork. The critique of *this* card answered the fork question explicitly: "SHOULD IT BECOME A SEPARATE CANDIDATE? NO — it is the same question done more identifiably; it is the revision, not a fork." The critic's round 2 cites no new fact that changed between the critique and now. The only new event is my admission of the demotion. If admission itself triggers re-registration, the loop teaches proposers to defend inflated rungs to protect their ledger slot — a perverse incentive worth refusing on principle. Speed of demotion should be treated like speed of concession: a feature.

3. **The idea-003 test distinguishes replacement from repair, and this passes it.** Idea 003 died because its amendments "achieved feasibility by abandoning the intervention question entirely" — the *experiment* changed until the motivating measurement was gone. Here the opposite happened: the experiment got sharper (image-space intervention, two shams, one designed specifically to isolate arterial from parenchymal calcification) and every asset, confound, keystone, and the motivating observation — a cardiovascular imaging marker and a breast-cancer score computed from the same pixels, never connected in print — are intact. What was removed is an interpretive clause the design was never going to earn, which is precisely what the rung ladder exists to gate.

I do not expect this to move the critic, and the disagreement is now genuinely irreducible — but it should be recorded at its true size: both parties endorse the same experiment, the same preregistered endpoint, and the same prohibited conclusions. The dispute is whether the ledger row keeps its number. That is a bookkeeping decision the human makes at shortlist; either resolution runs the identical Stage 0.

**What is lost:** nothing further beyond round 1's losses, plus the calibration sentence, which round 1 was not entitled to. Amendment count: still one substantive amendment; this round's concession narrows motivation wording, not the design.

**Spin-off:** none. If the human sides with the critic on registration, the amended card re-registers under the round-1 title with no scientific change.

**Status:** IRREDUCIBLE DISAGREEMENT
