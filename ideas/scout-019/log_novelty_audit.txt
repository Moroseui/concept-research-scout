The three findings that matter most:

1. **C1 is demoted to INCREMENTAL.** Kim et al. 2020 (Scientific Reports, DOI 10.1038/s41598-020-61519-9) — not cited in the card — already erased the entire retinal vasculature by inpainting and measured the sex-prediction AUC drop (0.93-class down to 0.682). So the rung-1 claim "the classifier uses the vessels" is published with an effect size. The candidate's branch-selective, area-matched dissociation of richness-vs-area genuinely survives as unclaimed, but the card's "missing experiment" framing and its interest/regret scores were computed against a mystery that is partly solved; it needs reframing before shortlisting. (The card's suspicious-looking 2026 DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-53485-5 checked out as real via Crossref.)

2. **C4's card has a citation error.** PMID 35923880 is a plant circadian-clock paper, and the segmentation author is Sexauer, not "Ebert." The correct anchor is Sexauer et al., Invest Radiol 2022, PMID 35797580 / PMC9390225 (the PMCID in the card was right). That paper also validated only on NLST/PleThora, so CT-RATE transfer is fully untested. The core novelty claim held: no fixed-volume shape intervention on any classifier was found anywhere — existing counterfactual methods all co-vary fluid amount with shape.

3. **The audit resolved several of the cards' open unverified claims.** C3's primary azygos-congestion citation is now pinned (Preger et al., Radiology 1969, PMID 4898452, azygos width vs CVP, r≈0.8), but no automated azygos localizer exists — consistent with its feasibility score of 2. C5's hiatus-measurement citation is pinned (Ouyang et al., Surg Endosc 2016, PMID 26304104), but every validated hiatus measurement is a manual MPR tracing and no automatic crural segmenter exists, so its measurement arm would be de novo; EXACT (arXiv 2604.24146) also reports CT-CLIP's hiatal-hernia head is weak (AUROC 0.66 on RAD-ChestCT), which bears on whether there's enough signal to attribute. For C2, Merlin's weights are confirmed publicly released, and steatosis appears in an independent benchmark's Merlin evaluation (CT-IDP), though the released finding vocabulary itself remains uninspected — the `NOT_INSPECTED` keystone cap stands correctly.
