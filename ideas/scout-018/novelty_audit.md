# Novelty audit — scouting cycle 018

## C1 — Breast arterial calcification inside Mirai, re-entered on its own terms (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) *Using Explainable AI to Characterize Features in the Mirai Mammographic Breast Cancer Risk Prediction Model*, Radiology: Artificial Intelligence, 2025, DOI **10.1148/ryai.240417**: linked Mirai features to annotated mammographic observations and found that a calcification-feature-only decoder retained five-year risk performance; radiologist review included benign calcifications, but the study did not isolate arterial calcification or intervene on BAC pixels. (ii) Yala et al., *Towards Robust Mammography-Based Models for Breast Cancer Risk*, Science Translational Medicine, 2021, DOI **10.1126/scitranslmed.aba4373**: introduced and externally tested Mirai without a BAC-specific mechanism audit. (iii) Lunit et al., *Detection and quantification of breast arterial calcifications on mammograms: a deep learning approach*, European Radiology, 2023, DOI **10.1007/s00330-023-09668-z**: automated BAC detection and burden estimation for cardiovascular use, not attribution of a breast-cancer risk model.
2. **Delta.** The candidate performs a BAC-specific, within-image removal-versus-sham intervention on frozen Mirai; the closest Mirai explanation study established use of a broad calcification feature family but did not distinguish arterial from ductal/other benign calcification or test causal score response to BAC removal.
3. **Why not done — BLIND_SPOT.** Mirai interpretation has been framed around cancer-associated lesion features, whereas BAC work is framed around cardiovascular opportunistic screening; the disciplinary boundary left arterial calcium unresolved inside the broader “benign calcification” result.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE.** Searches covered Mirai explanation/occlusion work, BAC segmentation and quantification, and combined Mirai–BAC terms. No BAC-specific Mirai intervention was found. This is a narrow delta, and DOI 10.1148/ryai.240417 must be treated as the closest and potentially claim-shrinking neighbor—not as proof that BAC itself is used.

## C2 — The dilated airways inside the fibrosis score (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) *Automated interstitial lung abnormalities detection at CT: external validation and potential recognition of traction bronchiectasis/bronchiolectasis*, Japanese Journal of Radiology, 2025, DOI **10.1007/s11604-025-01917-z**: showed that an ILA model score increased with reader-rated traction bronchiectasis severity, an observational association rather than a component-isolating intervention. (ii) Kuo et al., *Airway tapering: an objective image biomarker for bronchiectasis*, European Radiology, 2020, DOI **10.1007/s00330-019-06606-w**: defined automated airway-taper measurements, supplying an X measure but testing no fibrosis classifier. (iii) Walsh et al., *Deep learning for classifying fibrotic lung disease on high-resolution computed tomography: a case-cohort study*, Lancet Respiratory Medicine, 2018, DOI **10.1016/S2213-2600(18)30286-8**: classified fibrotic patterns at radiologist-level performance without separating traction bronchiectasis from reticulation as the used cue.
2. **Delta.** The candidate bidirectionally changes airway caliber while holding surrounding reticulation fixed to attribute one report-supervised CT-CLIP fibrosis score to traction bronchiectasis rather than merely correlating a fibrosis/ILA score with traction severity.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** The combination of a released volumetric report-supervised CT model (CT-CLIP/CT-RATE), public airway-tree segmentation/taper tools, and an already operational local inference pipeline makes a component-level intervention practical.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE.** Multi-source searches found automated traction quantification, fibrosis classifiers, and a recent observational model-score association, but no airway-caliber intervention at fixed reticulation. The 2025 association paper is close enough that the claim must remain “causal component attribution,” not first evidence linking AI fibrosis scores to traction bronchiectasis.

## C3 — The silhouette sign inside the consolidation score (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) *RoentMod: a synthetic chest X-ray modification model to identify and correct image interpretation model shortcuts*, Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2026, PMID **41792409**: generated realistic pathology counterfactuals and tested off-target shortcut responses in TorchXRayVision and foundation models, but did not contrast border-touching with border-sparing consolidation. (ii) Rajpurkar et al.-adjacent public-model work represented by Cohen et al., *TorchXRayVision: A library of chest X-ray datasets and models*, PMLR 172, 2022, arXiv **2111.00595**: released the consolidation heads and standardized evaluation, without a silhouette-sign mechanism test. (iii) *Automated, Standardized, Quantitative Analysis of Cardiovascular Borders on Chest X-Rays Using Deep Learning*, Journal of Imaging Informatics in Medicine, 2025, PMCID **PMC12102525**: quantified cardiovascular borders but did not test whether a consolidation classifier uses border obliteration.
2. **Delta.** The candidate holds synthetic opacity burden and vertical location fixed while varying only contact with a heart/diaphragm border, then corroborates with opacity-matched natural cases; existing counterfactual CXR work edits whole named pathologies or tests cross-pathology shortcuts.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** RoentMod-style anatomically constrained CXR editing plus public CheXmask contours and reproducible TorchXRayVision checkpoints now permit a placement-controlled rather than saliency-only test.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE.** Searches spanning “silhouette sign,” consolidation CNNs, cardiac-border quantification, and synthetic CXR counterfactuals found no quantitative border-contact attribution experiment. RoentMod materially reduces the engineering novelty, but not the scientific contrast.

## C4 — The healed granulomas inside lung-cancer risk (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) Sobieski et al., *Auditing Sybil: Explaining Deep Lung Cancer Risk Prediction Through Generative Interventional Attributions*, 2026, arXiv **2602.02560**: removed and inserted pulmonary nodules with diffusion bridges to audit Sybil, finding nodule contributions, interactions, artifacts, and radial bias; its full text contains no calcification-specific experiment. (ii) Mikhael et al., *Sybil: A Validated Deep Learning Model to Predict Future Lung Cancer Risk From a Single Low-Dose Chest Computed Tomography*, Journal of Clinical Oncology, 2023, DOI **10.1200/JCO.22.01345**: introduced Sybil and examined performance with/without visible future-cancer nodules, not benign calcification semantics. (iii) Shen et al., *An Interpretable Deep Hierarchical Semantic Convolutional Neural Network for Lung Nodule Malignancy Classification*, 2019, PMCID **PMC6623975**: predicted semantic nodule attributes including calcification alongside malignancy, but did not intervene on calcium or study Sybil’s patient-level future-risk score.
2. **Delta.** The candidate uses opposite-direction intranodular calcium edits to test whether Sybil assigns a specifically benign sign to calcified granulomas; the closest Sybil audit edits nodule presence and morphology, not calcium content or its signed benign meaning.
3. **Why not done — NEW_CAPABILITY.** The newly available S(H)NAP-style 3D nodule intervention framework demonstrates that realistic object-level Sybil audits are possible; adapting it to controlled calcium-density substitution creates a test that saliency and whole-nodule deletion could not identify.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_HIGH_CONFIDENCE.** A direct full-text check of the closest 2026 Sybil intervention paper found no calcification term, and broader searches found semantic calcification models but no signed calcium intervention in Sybil. Novelty is therefore specific to benign-calcification semantics, not to interventional auditing of Sybil generally.

## C5 — The heart failure written on the body wall (baseline)

1. **Neighbors.** (i) *Weakly supervised learning for subcutaneous edema segmentation of abdominal CT using pseudo-labels and multi-stage nnU-Nets*, Scientific Reports, 2024, PMCID **PMC11450639**: automated subcutaneous edema segmentation, but on abdominal CT and without probing a pleural-effusion classifier. (ii) *Simple quantitative chest CT for pulmonary edema*, Journal of Thoracic Imaging, 2020, PMCID **PMC7607389**: related lung attenuation measurements to pulmonary edema severity, measuring intrapulmonary rather than body-wall water and testing no model mechanism. (iii) *Automated Detection, Segmentation, and Classification of Pleural Effusion From Computed Tomography Scans Using Machine Learning*, Journal of Digital Imaging, 2022, PMCID **PMC9390225**: detected and characterized pleural fluid directly, without testing reliance on extrathoracic edema.
2. **Delta.** The candidate asks whether a report-supervised pleural-effusion score causally reads subcutaneous edema outside the pleural space, using repeat-session screening followed by a compartment-only attenuation intervention; neighbors measure edema or effusion themselves.
3. **Why not done — BLIND_SPOT.** Pleural-effusion algorithms are evaluated as organ-local detectors, while body-composition/edema methods treat the subcutaneous compartment as a separate quantification problem; neither field is incentivized to test cross-compartment shortcut use.
4. **Verdict — NO_DUPLICATE_FOUND_LIMITED_SEARCH.** Searches found no duplicate or close model-attribution study, but the edema-sensitive subcutaneous-attenuation premise was weakly indexed and the exact body-composition literature could not be exhaustively resolved from accessible primary full texts. Human verification should precede any novelty claim.
