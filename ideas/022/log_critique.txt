Critique complete: **PAUSE**, written to `ideas/022/critique.md` and committed (`b0a9d26`), following the repo's per-stage convention.

**The fatal objection:** the card's study object — a frozen, performant ISLES'24 model that consumes raw 4D CTP — does not exist as an obtainable artifact. The keystone screen had already shown the published winner (arXiv:2505.18424v2, Table 1) consumes derived maps only. My new legwork found the one public raw-4D-CTP entry, [ISLES24-PrediCTP](https://github.com/kimberly-amador/ISLES24-PrediCTP) ([Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81101-2_9)): it releases training code but **no checkpoint**, reports **Dice 0.20 / lesion-wise F1 0.02**, and leaves temporal padding semantics undocumented. Every workaround damages the claim — auditing the winner collapses the delta into the already-published map-level truncation literature (Kasasbeh, Copen), and self-training is quietly circular, since whether short prefixes are in-distribution would be decided by the experimenter's own augmentation choices. Not repairable without changing the question, so PAUSE rather than revision, with a concrete unblock: an obtainable frozen raw-CTP checkpoint with documented temporal semantics (author correspondence with the PrediCTP group is the realistic path).

Secondary findings: the weak-model problem also inverts the card's claimed decisive negative into a type-3 uninterpretable null (a Dice-0.20 model shrugging off censoring proves nothing); the correct kill-code is DATA_ACCESS, not the card's self-diagnosed IDENTIFIABILITY_FAILURE; and I verified the Bathla citation ([Stroke, 10.1161/STROKEAHA.121.034266](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/STROKEAHA.121.034266)) — the card characterizes it fairly — while adding a missed neighbor ([PLOS ONE truncation-detection, 10.1371/journal.pone.0283610](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0283610)) that usefully supplies citable instrumentation for the card's X.

**The constructive part:** the nearest defensible question needs no model at all — a dataset-level audit of whether terminal-curve incompleteness correlates with infarct severity and center across the 149 released ISLES'24 cases. It's deterministic curve arithmetic, lands on the charter's "identification of a decisive confound" success mode, doubles as the missing duration census (resolving the paused card's keystone clause (a) as a by-product), and per the claim-identity rule should register as a separate candidate with `parent_ids: [idea-022]`. Worth doing as a bounded Stage-0-style audit, conditional on confirmed registration access and a non-degenerate completeness census.
