**Verdict: PASS.** The keystone screen for idea 034 is written to `ideas/034/keystone_screen.md`, and the finding is stronger than the card dared to assume: the sensor-coverage mismatch is not just plausible — it was reported on the challenge's own forum during the competition, and the organizers confirmed they deliberately score the uncovered voxels.

What the investigation established:

- **The phenomenon is real in this release.** An official ISLES'24 Grand Challenge forum thread ("How will lesion voxels outside the field of view of the CTP imaging be handled?") states that "for a number of patients in ISLES'24, the field of view captured by the CTP is smaller than that of the DWI," and gives a concrete training case: patient 0019 has 1,685 lesion voxels on slices 58–60 outside the CTP image extents, where the derived maps are "completely empty." The organizers replied that they are **not masking those voxels out** — so the official metrics score terrain the perfusion sensor never saw, which is exactly the idea's premise. One provenance caveat is recorded: the forum page returns 403 to anonymous access and has no Wayback snapshot, so the quotes come from search-index content of the thread (confirmed by an exact-phrase query); a registered user should load the page once at the feasibility stage to harden this.
- **The papers genuinely don't document it.** I extracted the [dataset paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11142) (published in [Radiology: AI](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.250603)), the [challenge paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10966), and the [winning-team preprocessing paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18424v1) — none states CTP z-coverage, confirming the card's blind-spot claim. The scanner list (which includes a 64-row Philips Brilliance 64) is quoted in the memo as supporting inference only.
- **Stage 1 got cheaper.** The full 149-case training set is now openly downloadable without challenge registration ([Zenodo record 16813698](https://zenodo.org/records/16813698), CC BY-NC-SA, 99 GB), with a [Hugging Face mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hugging-science/isles24-stroke) — easier access than the card assumed.

What remains open, honestly recorded: the quantitative half of the keystone (overflow > 5% of lesion volume in ≥ 10% of cases) is unmeasured — that is precisely the prespecified stage-1 census, not a screen failure.
